NAME dock-approach
CLAIM A closing rate inside the pair's own gate mates it, and the weld keeps the gap inside that gate.
KIND invariant
# --dock-test approach puts the two ports 0.55 m apart on the station's port AXIS -- face
# gap 0.30 m -- and gives the probe 0.09 m/s along it. The r1 pair captures inside 0.10 m of
# face gap at 0.15 m/s or slower (#1152), so the mate lands about 2.2 s in, measured.
# The fixture used to fire the 50 kN engine for 30 ms instead. That arrived at 0.51 m/s --
# over three times the cap -- and the case was at the mercy of how many physics substeps a
# wall-clock press happened to catch (the flake this used to document). No keys are pressed
# here, so the mate has exactly one cause: the approach.
# The d=/cap= pair is the ticket. absorbShip is a rigid rebase, so whatever face gap the
# capture tick measures is the air that stays between the ports forever; 1.5 m of gate used
# to mean a docked pair holding each other at arm's length. So the pin is not "d starts with
# 0.0" -- it is d < cap, with cap read off the same row: the gate's own distance for this
# pair, printed since #1152. A digit pattern sat 0.5 mm from the boundary and went red at
# --physics-hz 120, where a coarser tick lets the pair cross in bigger steps and d prints
# 0.100. Comparing the two figures says what is actually claimed, and the cap > 0.05 floor
# keeps the comparison from being vacuous.
ARGS --dock-test approach --time-accel 1 --timeout 8
EXPECT Dock-test (approach)
EXPECT [dock] t=
EXPECT a="dockprobe" b="dockstation"
CHECK (lambda x: len(x) == 1 and 0.05 < float(x[0][1]) and float(x[0][0]) < float(x[0][1]))(re.findall(r'\[dock\] t=\S+ a="dockprobe" b="dockstation" d=([-\d.]+) m cap=([-\d.]+) m v=', out))
