NAME elevator-trim
CLAIM The same 569 kg skiff, with 12 deg of elevator trim authored on the parts and nothing else changed, holds +4.8..+5.8 deg of angle of attack through its phugoid with the stick released: the download that trims this airframe comes from deflection, and the standing bill is real (0.33-0.43 m2 of induced CdA while the hull is pointing into the flow, 0.82 the instant the rudder is kicked). The gravity-keyed bed spawns it at 80 m/s of GROUND speed, and Kerbin's column is worth 31.6 m/s at that band -- 19 deg off the spawn heading -- so the hull arrives with 52 m/s of air and this run stays inside its envelope and settles back after the kick.
KIND golden
ARGS --time-accel 1 --startship skiff_trim,res/ships/skiff_trim.json,Kerbin,flying-cruise
# --no-paint: every assertion here parses [drag]/[mass] rows, so the 3D pass buys
# nothing and costs 95% of a scripted frame (#666).
ARGS --sim-steps 3000 --drag-log --mass-log --no-paint
ARGS --sim-press 500,3000,R
ARGS --sim-press 500,20000,T
ARGS --sim-press 25000,1000,A
EXPECT [drag]
EXPECT [mass]
CHECK len(drag) >= 40
# THE HOLD, up to the kick (t < 25 s): 3.9..4.7 deg, every row nose-high, bought by a tab
# and not by a stick.
# What this case carried into #686, kept because it is the reason the tab is where it is:
# #568 moved the hold 2 deg up and 20 m/s down -- the pod-on-tank stack carries its
# tailplane pair 1.65 m behind the wing rather than the spindle's 0.85 m, so the same 12
# deg of tab buys more download than this hull wants. #567 widened the exchange rather
# than shifting it (floor 0.2 deg lower, top 0.3 higher, the bottom of the exchange 5.4
# m/s slower, 28.8 -> 23.4), because the trim download now has 24 m2 of AR-10.7 wing to
# answer it. #543 A5 (compressibility) widened it again: at the fast end of that phugoid
# (78.1 m/s, Mach 0.284 in Kerbin's 275.0 m/s air) the printed CLa read 174.54 against
# the 168.0 the parts author, and at the top of the arc (21.7 m/s, Mach 0.079) 167.51 --
# a 4.2% difference the old law could not make, and it pushed the bottom of the exchange
# from 6.2 to 6.7 deg because the stiffer fast end answers harder there.
# #686 moved the air, and the hold came down and flattened with it. The bed sits at 8.5 km
# now (0.1 x an 85 km top, #682) in a troposphere instead of one isothermal 188 K column:
# rho 0.480-0.503 kg/m3, T 231-234 K, a 305-307 m/s.
# Kerbin carrying its own column moved the arc as well: the ground speed runs 51.6..81.9
# m/s while the AIR one runs 23.8..54.6, because this bed keys to the body's circular speed
# and never to its air (#693) and the spawn heads 19 deg off a 31.6 m/s tailwind. Fast end:
# Mach 0.176, CLa 170.05. Slow end: Mach 0.077, CLa 167.70. So the band tilts back the other
# way -- it peaks at the slow end (5.8 deg at t=15) and sags to 4.8 at the fast -- which is
# what A5's slope law does to an air 28 m/s slower than the unwinded bed handed it. This
# case pins that a 12 deg tab still buys ONE band across the exchange; why the band tilts is
# not re-litigated here.
CHECK all(4.7 < d["aoa"] < 5.9 for d in drag if d["aoa"] is not None and d["t"] < 25.0)
# The exchange, pre-kick: 51.6..81.9 m/s of GROUND speed (23.8..54.6 of air) inside 313 m
# of height, and the whole 50 s run stays inside that. This floor is NOT a stall floor, and
# that is worth saying out loud:
# res/ships/README.md's 23.1 m/s is arithmetic at low Mach (5.45 kN of weight over the
# authored slope at the wing's 20.1 deg stall angle) in the 7 km air at rho 0.346 that
# number was written against. The same arithmetic in #686's air prints 17.7 m/s back at
# 7 km (rho 0.594, which is the row README carries now) and near 19 m/s up HERE, at the
# 8.5 km bed in rho 0.502 -- the two differ by 1/sqrt(rho), not by a law. The
# slow rows are not equilibrium flight anyway: at 23.8 m/s of air -- 51.6 m/s over the
# ground, the difference being the column -- the hull bills |L| = 1.75 kN against a 5.6 kN
# weight, which is a hull trading speed for height rather than one
# holding it. (Every |L| quoted in this file is post-#699 and says HULL, not WING: the
# column now carries the tab's download too, so the same rows used to read 2.4 kN here,
# 28.98 kN at the fast end and 43 N in the departure this bed used to start.) What the floor
# guards is the arc
# punching clean through the bottom of the exchange -- a wing whose slope DROPPED would
# fly a shallower one, and this row would
# rise -- so it sits 1.6 m/s under the slowest pre-kick row rather than being tuned to it.
# With the air moving, read that floor for what it is: |v| is the GROUND speed, so it can
# never fall below the column, and the wing's own bottom is the 23.8 m/s of air above.
CHECK min(d["v"] for d in drag if d["t"] < 25.0) > 50.0
CHECK max(d["alt_asl"] for d in drag) - min(d["alt_asl"] for d in drag) < 450.0
# THE DEPARTURE THAT STOPPED HAPPENING. Unwinded, this bed spawned the hull at 80 m/s of air
# against a near 41 m/s trimmed glide -- 1.9x -- so the zoom climb that answers it ate the
# speed down and the A press at 25 s put the hull into a fall: 38.7 m/s on the way down at
# t=25, 5.75 m/s and 40.6 deg nose-down four seconds later, -54.9 with Beta -14.6 at t=31,
# billing 48 N of lift against a 5.58 kN weight. Those two pins are gone and the reason is
# the last sentence of the note above: the bed keys to the body's circular speed, so Kerbin
# taking up a 31.6 m/s column at that band took ~28 m/s off the air the hull arrived with
# (52 m/s against a 41 m/s glide: 1.27x, not 1.9x). What this run still shows is a hull that
# leaves trim inside the envelope -- 5.8 deg, 0.82 m2 of induced the instant the rudder is
# kicked -- and settles back on its own. What it no longer shows is a departure, and it will
# not show one again until that bed stops being gravity-keyed (#693).
CHECK all(d["aoa"] > 4.5 for d in drag if d["aoa"] is not None and d["t"] >= 34.0)
CHECK min(d["v"] for d in drag if d["t"] >= 34.0) > 25.0
# 12 deg of standing deflection, and the bill follows the command: the rows that point
# into the flow before the kick bill 0.33-0.43 m2 of induced. The history of that number
# is the history of the airframe: #567 took 0.13 m2 off the hold and 0.26 off the kick row
# when the span went 12 m -> 16 m, because inducedCd charges a lifting system
# (cl x A)^2 / (pi x 0.8 x span^2); #543 A5 put 0.02-0.07 m2 back on the hold by pricing
# the CL its slope law had just made. #686 took the hold from 0.34-0.54 to 0.27-0.35 --
# the arc asks less of the wing at the top of its swing than the old one did -- and the
# kick row from 0.94 to 0.80 (t=26, Beta -7.1). Kerbin's column moved the band up by
# 0.06-0.08 m2 (0.333..0.431, t=26 now 0.823 at Beta -7.3): the same tab, flown through air
# 28 m/s slower than this bed used to hand it, is asked for more of its lift.
# The kick rows (|Beta| >= 4) sit outside the band on purpose: they run 0.50-0.82, the top
# being the t=26 row a breath after the kick (Beta -7.3, rudder still off neutral). A
# steered surface pays induced drag at its own mount (#494), which is what the max row
# below is for; it no longer has to be a clean row to prove it.
# Pinned on the PITCH axis, because this run also kicks the rudder (Beta swings -7.3..5.4)
# and an unpinned "any surface is well off neutral" would pass on that alone.
CHECK all(0.30 < d["induced"] < 0.45 for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None and d.get("beta") is not None and abs(d["beta"]) < 4.0 and d["t"] < 25.0)
# ... and on the OTHER side of the departure. The `t < 25.0` cutoff above stops at the
# kick, which is also what would hide a drift in the half this run reaches after recovery:
# the pre-kick band says nothing once the airframe has come back, and its rows would still
# pass at 0.39. So read the settled tail too: from t=40 s on the tab bills 0.333..0.390 m2
# (11 rows, |Beta| < 4), the same standing bill it carried INTO the excursion. "It
# recovers" is pinned above on AoA and speed; this says it recovers TRIMMED, with the
# trim download the tab has been paying all along still the one paying it.
CHECK all(0.30 < d["induced"] < 0.42 for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None and d.get("beta") is not None and abs(d["beta"]) < 4.0 and d["t"] >= 40.0)
CHECK max(d["induced"] for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None) > 0.75
# Ld= (#694): the air's force SPLIT ABOUT THE FLOW -- its component perpendicular to the
# air-relative flow over its component along it -- which is the ratio a glide ratio equals.
# Through the hold it reads 6.64..7.28, median 7.17. The same hull in a settled 200 s
# engines-off glide prints 7.19 against the 7.28 its own ground track measures (41.20 m/s over
# 5.66 m/s of sink) -- this row is not the glide and does not claim to be. Pinned on the hold,
# where the rows point into the flow; the departure rows sit outside it on purpose -- by t=29
# the hull is falling at 0.8 and the ratio means little.
# #699 is why the row now agrees with itself. |L| used to leave the control loop's deflection
# force out of the sum, and on this 12 deg-tabbed hull that is 13 % of the force holding it up:
# the log printed MORE LIFT THAN THE AIR'S ENTIRE FORCE on 47 of these 50 rows (|L|/|F| up to
# 1.22), and the ratio built off it -- |L| over the bills this line prints, (Cd*A + Induced +
# Profile) x q -- came out 8.24..8.80, median 8.68, which is the 8.6 res/ships/README.md's
# skiff row still quotes. Measured on this run since #699:
#   * |L| over the CHARGED bills, q x (1.2 CdA + Induced + Profile): 6.67..7.27, median 7.17 --
#     this row's Ld, to the digit. The multiplier matters: A= and Cd= print the RAW hull product
#     while the force charges drag_cd x them (1.2 by default; Induced= and Profile= already
#     carry it). At t=1 the two bill sets are 2096 N and 2239 N against 2236 N of measured
#     along-flow force, and at --drag-cd 1 they agree to the watt (1898 N both).
#   * |L| over the RAW printed bills: 7.14..7.76, median 7.66 -- the same statement 6 % high
#     because it skips that multiplier.
# So README's 8.6 is stale twice over now: it predates #699 and it reads the raw bills. Which
# of these it should lead with is still #694's open call, not this case's.
# The bound that would have caught #699: over EVERY row of this run -- hold, kick, fall
# and recovery -- the lift part never outweighs the whole aero force it came from.
# Pre-#699, 47 of these 50 rows broke it, by up to 22 %. Treat it as a tell rather than a
# law: each surface stands in its OWN flow (#483), so a hull pitching hard enough for
# w x r to matter leaks across the split, and this airframe's margin is only ~1 % at
# Ld 7.17 (worst row here 0.9912). A hull with a much better L/D could reach |L| == |F|
# at two decimals -- if that happens, do NOT widen this band; compare the logged force
# VECTORS (lastForceVecs' kForceLift / kForceCtrl) instead of a sum of magnitudes.
CHECK all(d["L"] <= d["F"] for d in drag)
# Not vacuous: |L| is an optional column in the parser, so a row that stopped printing it
# would make the CHECK above agree with nothing (#653).
CHECK len([d for d in drag if d.get("L") is not None and d.get("F") is not None]) == len(drag)
CHECK all(6.5 < d["ld"] < 7.5 for d in drag if d.get("ld") is not None and d.get("beta") is not None and abs(d["beta"]) < 4.0 and d["t"] < 25.0)
# Not vacuous: every row in this run prints a numbered Ld, because the air and the flow
# are there the whole time (the dash means no flow to score, or the air PUSHING the hull).
CHECK len([d for d in drag if d.get("ld") is not None]) == len(drag)
CHECK any(c["axis"] == "pitch" for c in ctrl)
CHECK all(11.5 < abs(c["deg"]) < 12.5 for c in ctrl if c["axis"] == "pitch")
# The tail is one lifting system with its elevators (#556): its span is 4 m, so
# the standing bill is priced at AR 3.1, not at one 1.6 m tab.
