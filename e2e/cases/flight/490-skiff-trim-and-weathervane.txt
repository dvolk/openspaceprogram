NAME skiff-trim-and-weathervane
CLAIM The skiff is the airframe the aero design point was written for: 568 kg on 24 m2 of AR-10.7 wing, a command pod stacked on a jet fuel tank, spawned at 80 m/s and 8.5 km, given full throttle for 20 s and then left alone: it trades that excess through one phugoid of 55..109 m/s and 8449..8976 m while its angle of attack never leaves +2.4..+3.4 deg, and settles back to +2.4..+2.5 with +0.55 m of static margin and the hull's along-flow bill BEHIND its balance point on every row. One second of rudder puts 5.1 deg of sideslip on it and the weathervane has it back inside 0.2 deg by t=36 s. On cruise rows the hull bill is Cd 0.37-0.38 on 0.80-0.81 m2, where the smooth spindle this replaced paid 0.11 on 0.87.
KIND golden
# #568 put the pod back on a tank: capsule_r0.5h1.5 at the root, jet_tank_r0.4h2 1.75 m
# aft of it, the engine 3.25 m aft, wings on the TANK at 1.2 m aft of the pod's centre,
# tail 2.85 m, elevators 3.65 m. #570 hung the rudder off the fin's trailing edge at
# 3.95 m and the ailerons off the wings' at 2.45 m -- clear plates, not plates buried
# in their parents. #567 then grew that wing to 16 x 1.5 (its chord now spans 1.95..0.45
# m, the ailerons hang off its trailing edge at 2.30 m aft of the origin, tips 8.4 m
# out; the model prices the pair as one 16.00 m system) and the hull to 569 kg (472 of
# parts, tanks full, plus one 97 kg kerbal).
# #1171 then weighed every part off its own mesh instead of the cylinder around it, and
# 121.87 kg of the 122 kg the skiff lost was the POD alone -- a shell weighs less than
# the solid it replaced. Since #945 credits each strip of the silhouette union to the
# upwind-most part covering it, the along-flow push is the POD's frontal disc, so losing
# 122 kg at the nose walked the COM aft and moved every number on this row: SM +0.55 ->
# +0.31 and FormCp -0.11 -> +0.17, i.e. the hull's drag crossed to in FRONT of the
# balance point and started levering alpha instead of weathervaning. That is #1203, and
# for a stretch this case pinned only |FormCp| <= 0.25 m and a 3.5 s burn, because on a
# 446 kg hull the 20 s burn below departs the airframe (AoA 18.5 with the kick, 58.4 /
# Beta -43.9 with no press at all -- tmp/night10/g2_nopress.log) and bands over a
# departure are a run, not a law.
# WHAT PUT IT BACK: POD_OUTFIT_KG in utils/art/gen_parts.py (#1204's interim) -- a
# declared allowance for the seat, panel, life support and drums that a closed shell
# mesh cannot enclose, 121.87 kg on capsule_r0.5h1.5. The launch mass, the margin and
# the sign of FormCp are the pre-#1171 ones again, so the 20 s burn is back: on a
# balanced hull it stays in attached flow the whole 50 s (Mach <= 0.27, 16.6 deg clear
# of the wing's 20.05 deg stall peak), which is what lets the bands below measure trim
# and damping again. The per-seat law (how much, at which position, without
# double-counting the occupant) is #1204; the mass pin is the interim's number.
ARGS --time-accel 1 --startship skiff,res/ships/skiff.json,Kerbin,flying-cruise
# --no-paint: this case reads [drag] rows, never pixels, and a scripted frame
# without the 3D pass costs 0.12 ms rather than 3.08 (#666). The rows are the
# same either way; the run is 5x cheaper.
ARGS --sim-steps 3000 --drag-log --mass-log --no-paint
ARGS --sim-press 500,3000,R
ARGS --sim-press 500,20000,T
ARGS --sim-press 25000,1000,A
EXPECT [drag]
EXPECT [mass]
CHECK len(drag) >= 40
# Trim, held: the trim is a wing station and a wing-loading, not a stick. Pin it on the
# window where the hull has stopped exchanging energy (t >= 40 s): +2.4..+2.5 deg there.
# Kerbin's wind column is still worth ~31 m/s at this 8.5 km bed, and a weathervaning
# hull's null attitude in moving air is not its null attitude in still air.
CHECK all(2.2 < d["aoa"] < 2.8 for d in drag if d["t"] >= 40.0 and d["aoa"] is not None)
# Over ALL rows, 15 deg clear of the wing's stall peak (parts.json stall_angle
# 0.35 rad = 20.05 deg). Measured 2.4..3.4, the 3.4 being the apex of the one exchange
# at 55.4 m/s. Two bands rather than one stretched over both: the settled trim above is
# the law and this envelope is the case noticing if the exchange starts running away.
# On the 446 kg hull this same rig read 18.5 with the kick and 58.4 with no press, which
# is the shape #1203 was filed about.
CHECK all(2.0 < d["aoa"] < 5.0 for d in drag if d["aoa"] is not None)
# The exchange, once: the bed gives 80 m/s of GROUND speed and the column is worth
# ~31 m/s of it, so the hull arrives with roughly its own trim speed of air, the throttle
# adds the rest, and it trades that for height and back once. Measured 55.4 m/s at the
# 8976 m apex to 109.2 m/s in the dive, and it damps: by t=41 s the arc is 89 m/s
# (the alt band below says the same thing). Both floors guard the exchange running away
# further still.
CHECK min(d["v"] for d in drag) > 50.0
CHECK max(d["v"] for d in drag) < 115.0
# The bed, not the airframe: flying-cruise spawns at 0.1 x the body's air top, and #682
# moved that top 70 -> 85 km, so the spawn row is 8501 m ASL where it used to read 7005.
# This floor is the spawn altitude less the 50 m the hull may fall before its wings
# answer -- it tracks the bed, so it moves whenever the air top does. It is not a sink
# measurement: the lowest row of the first 20 s is 8473 m, because this bed spawns above
# trim and climbs rather than falling. What it catches is the bed moving (a 70 km top
# puts the spawn at 7002 and reddens this) and a hull that falls before its wings answer.
CHECK min(d["alt_asl"] for d in drag if d["t"] <= 20.0) > 8400.0
# ... and over the whole 50 s the phugoid stays inside 530 m of the bed: measured
# 8449..8976. The ceiling reddens if the exchange trades the hull up into thinner air
# (rho is printed on the row, and every band above is speed- and density-coupled).
CHECK min(d["alt_asl"] for d in drag) > 8400.0
CHECK max(d["alt_asl"] for d in drag) < 9050.0
# Static margin positive and steady (the VAB number, in flight): +0.55 on every one of
# the 50 rows, and buildStability() on the shipped file with one suit answers +0.5532.
# The band separates this from the same build with no crew (the bench answers +0.3714, so
# one suit adds 0.18 m) and from a rig that diverges.
CHECK all(d.get("sm") is not None for d in drag)
CHECK all(0.51 < d["sm"] < 0.59 for d in drag if d.get("sm") is not None)
# The kick: 5.1 deg of sideslip one second after the press, and inside 0.2 deg again
# from t=36 s on. Same 1 s press, same rudder; the number belongs to where in the
# phugoid it lands (here at 90 m/s of ground speed) and a decade-wide band on the peak
# is honest about that. What IS this case's claim is the shape after it -- Beta -0.2,
# -3.6, -5.1, -1.2, +2.7, +1.4, -1.7, -0.0, +0.8, -0.7, +0.4, -0.2 over the twelve rows
# after the press: an oscillation the weathervane (CpL +0.72..+0.83, SideCp -1.14..-1.22
# on those rows) is winning, crossing zero five times and shrinking each time.
CHECK 4.0 < max(abs(d["beta"]) for d in drag if d.get("beta") is not None) < 8.0
CHECK all(abs(d["beta"]) < 1.5 for d in drag if d["t"] >= 36.0)
# The bill, cruise rows: pod+tank+engine union 0.80..0.81 m2, which since #945 is the
# POD's alone (capsule drag_forward 0.35) rather than a mean of the stack's anchors
# (capsule 0.35, tank 1.0, engine 0.15 -- the 0.57-0.60 this used to read), because the
# tank and engine sit in the pod's shadow and vote for nothing.
# The predicate has to say which rows ARE cruise, and |Beta| <= 6 does not: this arc
# never exceeds 5.1 deg of sideslip or 3.4 of alpha, so that filter admits all fifty
# rows and a "cruise" band silently becomes an all-rows band -- which is how the ceilings
# below came out wider than the numbers this header quotes. |Beta| <= 1.0 excludes
# exactly the six rows of the kick's swing, which is the thing that makes the same stack
# present 0.85 m2 rather than 0.81. Those rows get their own ceiling, not the cruise
# band stretched until it tests nothing.
CHECK all(0.78 < d["a"] < 0.83 for d in drag if d.get("a") and abs(d["aoa"]) <= 6 and abs(d["beta"]) <= 1.0)
CHECK all(d["a"] < 0.90 for d in drag if d.get("a"))
# The CLAIM's Cd pair is pinned beside the area it weights: 0.37..0.38 on cruise rows,
# measured (0.565..0.601 while the whole stack voted). A bill is Cd x A, so pinning one
# without the other leaves half the claim untestable -- which is how this row stayed
# green through a 1.44x change in the air. The off-cruise rows read up to 0.41.
CHECK all(0.36 < d["cd"] < 0.40 for d in drag if abs(d["aoa"]) <= 6 and abs(d["beta"]) <= 1.0)
CHECK all(d["cd"] < 0.45 for d in drag)
# Wings at 0.010 profile: 0.45..0.48 m2 of profile bill on cruise rows, 0.50 at the
# apex where the wing is working hardest at 55.4 m/s.
CHECK all(0.43 < d["profile"] < 0.50 for d in drag if d.get("profile") is not None and abs(d["aoa"]) <= 6 and abs(d["beta"]) <= 1.0)
CHECK all(d["profile"] < 0.60 for d in drag if d.get("profile") is not None)
# None-guarded because #560 made Induced= dashable: every row of this run prints a
# number, but max() over a dict comprehension that can yield None is a TypeError waiting
# for the first row that does not.
# The floor is the claim "the wing is billed at all": smallest induced bill of this run
# prints 0.10 m2 (t=1 s, at 81 m/s where the wing is barely loaded). A ceiling with no
# floor stays green if the law stops charging -- an AR of zero short-circuits inducedCd
# in drag.h, and a pane that falls out of its lifting system is skipped by the bill --
# while every other band here (aoa, sm, profile, a) still passes. The cruise ceiling
# catches the other direction: a hangar-sized induced bill showing up as a wall in level
# flight. Worst row of the run is the kick at 0.43, where the rudder's own side force
# is being paid for.
CHECK all(d["induced"] > 0.08 for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None)
CHECK all(d["induced"] < 0.16 for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None and abs(d["aoa"]) <= 6 and abs(d["beta"]) <= 1.0)
CHECK all(d["induced"] < 0.50 for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None)
# #694's ratio, on the rows the CLAIM's glide language rests on: 4.95..6.78 across the
# whole run and 5.85..5.88 whenever the hull is settled. A lift/drag band is the one place
# where the wing's and the hull's bills have to agree with each other, so it reddens when
# either side's authored number moves on its own.
CHECK all(4.5 < d["ld"] < 8.0 for d in drag if d.get("ld") is not None)
# The sign, not just the magnitude: the hull's along-flow push must stay BEHIND the COM,
# "or the hull levers angle of attack instead of weathervaning it". #1171 put it in front
# (+0.17 m) by taking the pod's mass away, and this line went from a sign band to
# |lever| <= 0.25 m while that stood. #1204's allowance puts the nose back, so the sign
# band is back: measured -0.06..-0.10 m on all 50 rows, which is the pod's frontal disc
# sitting just aft of the balance point -- the design point #945 wrote. The margin under
# the floor is what reddens if the COM walks forward again for any reason (a heavier
# pod, a shorter stack, a seat that stops being counted).
# Two lines of action cannot fold into one scalar without lying about at least one of
# them: the perpendicular push gets its own SideCp= beside this.
CHECK all(-0.20 < d["formcp"] < 0.0 for d in drag if d.get("formcp") is not None)
# #947, pinned on the airframe its body was written against. This hull flies a degree or
# two off its line, so most rows have crossflow; the hull's strip is half-shielded by the
# pod ahead of it and bills the rest; and the normal force lands aft, doing the
# weathervane work FormCp alone could not. Measured 0.11..0.31 m2 at -1.14..-1.22 m.
# The floor guards "the law bills at all"; the band around -1.2 guards the SIGN, which is
# this case's claim made checkable in its own column -- and it is the one lever that did
# NOT flip when the COM walked.
CHECK all(d["sidecda"] is not None and d["sidecda"] > 0.05 for d in drag)
CHECK all(-1.4 < d["sidecp"] < -0.95 for d in drag if d.get("sidecp") is not None)
# The cause of every move above, pinned where it can be seen rather than inferred:
# 568.11 kg, of which the pod's own 215.59 (93.72 of shell + 121.87 of outfit, #1204).
CHECK any(m["ship"] == "skiff" and m["parts"] == 13 and abs(m["mass"] - 568.11) < 1.0 for m in mass)
