NAME skiff-trim-and-weathervane
CLAIM The skiff is the airframe the aero design point was written for: 446 kg on 24 m2 of AR-10.7 wing, a command pod stacked on a jet fuel tank, spawned at 80 m/s and 8.5 km given 3.5 s of throttle and then left alone: it swings +2.6..+7.9 deg of angle of attack through a phugoid that dies away to a settled +3.6..+3.9, with +0.31 m of static margin on every row. One second of rudder puts 7.5 deg of sideslip on it and the weathervane has it back inside 1.0 deg by t=36 s. On cruise rows the hull bill is Cd 0.37-0.43 on 0.80-0.87 m2, where the smooth spindle this replaced paid 0.11 on 0.87.
KIND golden
# #568 put the pod back on a tank: capsule_r0.5h1.5 at the root, jet_tank_r0.4h2 1.75 m
# aft of it, the engine 3.25 m aft, wings on the TANK at 1.2 m aft of the pod's centre,
# tail 2.85 m, elevators 3.65 m. #570 hung the rudder off the fin's trailing edge at
# 3.95 m and the ailerons off the wings' at 2.45 m -- clear plates, not plates buried
# in their parents. #567 then grew that wing to 16 x 1.5 (its chord now spans 1.95..0.45
# m, the ailerons hang off its trailing edge at 2.30 m aft of the origin, tips 8.4 m
# out; the model prices the pair as one 16.00 m system) and the hull to 569 kg (472 of
# parts, tanks full, plus one 97 kg kerbal). #1171 then weighed every part off its own
# mesh instead of the cylinder around it: the skiff launches at 446.24 kg (pinned below
# via [mass]) and 121.87 kg of the 122 kg difference is the POD alone, because a shell
# weighs less than the solid it replaced. Since #945 credits each strip of the silhouette
# union to the upwind-most part covering it, the along-flow push is the POD's frontal
# disc -- and with 122 kg gone from the nose the COM walked aft, which is what moved
# every number on this row. #1203 is that consequence; the mass pin is the cause.
# WHY THE BURN IS 3.5 s AND NOT 20 s. This row used to hold full throttle for 20 s, and
# the claim is trim plus weathervane, both of which are only laws while the hull flies
# attached. On the 446 kg hull a 20 s burn ends at 111 m/s at the bottom of the first
# exchange -- 2.7x the ~41 m/s it trims at -- and the next climb stalls at the apex:
# AoA 18.5 deg with the kick, and AoA 58.4 / Beta -43.9 with NO press at all (measured on
# this build; tmp/night10/g2_nopress.log). Whether it departs, and how far, turns on
# which way the phugoid happens to be leaning at t=32 s, so every band on that arc is a
# run, not a law -- which is what #1201 asks a re-pin to notice. A 3.5 s burn leaves the
# hull in attached flow for the whole 50 s (Mach <= 0.194, 12 deg clear of the wing's
# 20.05 deg stall peak), so the excursion bands below measure DAMPING, and the departure
# is left to #1203 to fix and re-test rather than pinned here as normal.
ARGS --time-accel 1 --startship skiff,res/ships/skiff.json,Kerbin,flying-cruise
# --no-paint: this case reads [drag] rows, never pixels, and a scripted frame
# without the 3D pass costs 0.12 ms rather than 3.08 (#666). The rows are the
# same either way; the run is 5x cheaper.
ARGS --sim-steps 3000 --drag-log --mass-log --no-paint
ARGS --sim-press 500,3000,R
ARGS --sim-press 500,4000,T
ARGS --sim-press 25000,1000,A
EXPECT [drag]
EXPECT [mass]
CHECK len(drag) >= 40
# Trim, held: the trim is a wing station and a wing-loading, not a stick. Pin it on the
# window where the hull has stopped exchanging energy (t >= 40 s): +3.6..+3.9 deg there,
# against the +2.5 the 569 kg hull settled at. (+0.31 m of static margin instead of
# +0.55 -- a weaker margin needs more alpha to hold the same nose.) Kerbin's wind column
# is still worth ~31 m/s at this 8.5 km bed, and a weathervaning hull's null attitude in
# moving air is not its null attitude in still air.
CHECK all(3.3 < d["aoa"] < 4.3 for d in drag if d["t"] >= 40.0 and d["aoa"] is not None)
# Over ALL rows, still 10 deg clear of the wing's stall peak (parts.json stall_angle
# 0.35 rad = 20.05 deg). Measured 2.6..7.9, the 7.9 being the first climb's apex where
# the hull is at 45.6 m/s -- the phugoid's alpha extremes are wider than the settled
# trim above, and both are pinned rather than one band stretched over both. On the old
# 20 s burn this row read 18.5 and, without the press, 58.4; the ceiling is where the
# case would notice the exchange starting to run away before #1203 is settled.
CHECK all(2.0 < d["aoa"] < 10.0 for d in drag if d["aoa"] is not None)
# The exchange, once: the bed gives 80 m/s of air and this hull trims near 41 m/s in
# #686's air, so it trades that excess for height and back. Measured 45.6 m/s at the
# 8695 m apex to 86.6 m/s in the dive, against 40.7..79.8 on the 569 kg hull: less mass
# to lift means the same excess energy buys a bigger exchange, and the throttle here is
# 3.5 s rather than 20. It damps -- by t=45 s the arc is 64..77 m/s (the alt band below
# says the same thing). Both floors guard the exchange running away further still.
CHECK min(d["v"] for d in drag) > 40.0
CHECK max(d["v"] for d in drag) < 95.0
# The bed, not the airframe: flying-cruise spawns at 0.1 x the body's air top, and #682
# moved that top 70 -> 85 km, so the spawn row is 8501 m ASL where it used to read 7005.
# This floor is the spawn altitude less the 50 m the hull may fall before its wings
# answer -- it tracks the bed, so it moves whenever the air top does. It is not a sink
# measurement: the lowest row of the first 20 s IS the spawn row (8501 m), because this
# bed spawns above trim and climbs rather than falling. What it catches is the bed moving
# (a 70 km top puts the spawn at 7002 and reddens this) and a hull that falls 50 m before
# its wings answer.
CHECK min(d["alt_asl"] for d in drag if d["t"] <= 20.0) > 8400.0
# ... and over the whole 50 s the phugoid stays inside 330 m of the bed: measured
# 8372..8695. The ceiling reddens if the exchange trades the hull up into thinner air
# (rho is printed on the row, and every band above is speed- and density-coupled).
CHECK min(d["alt_asl"] for d in drag) > 8300.0
CHECK max(d["alt_asl"] for d in drag) < 8750.0
# Static margin positive and steady (the VAB number, in flight): +0.31 on every one of
# the 50 rows, and buildStability() on the shipped file with one suit answers +0.3122.
# The band separates this from the same build with no crew (the bench answers -0.0001 for
# the empty skiff, so one suit is now the whole margin) and from a rig that diverges.
CHECK all(d.get("sm") is not None for d in drag)
CHECK all(0.28 < d["sm"] < 0.34 for d in drag if d.get("sm") is not None)
# The kick: 7.5 deg of sideslip one second after the press, and inside 1.0 deg again
# from t=36 s on. The old row read 5.5 deg and back inside 0.1 -- same 1 s press, same
# rudder; what changed is the speed it lands at (79 m/s here against 45 on #686's arc,
# and rudder torque is q x area) and, aft of the kick, the whole exchange being smaller
# than the one the 20 s burn used to start. The decade-wide band is honest: the number
# belongs to where in the phugoid the press lands, which is not this case's claim. What
# IS this case's claim is the shape after it -- Beta 7.5, 2.1, 5.5, 2.0, 3.1, 3.0, 0.2
# over the six rows after the press, an oscillation the weathervane (CpL +0.95..+1.06,
# SideCp -0.95..-1.03 on those rows) is winning.
CHECK 6.0 < max(abs(d["beta"]) for d in drag if d.get("beta") is not None) < 10.0
CHECK all(abs(d["beta"]) < 1.5 for d in drag if d["t"] >= 36.0)
# The bill, cruise rows: pod+tank+engine union 0.80..0.87 m2, which since #945 is the
# POD's alone (capsule drag_forward 0.35) rather than a mean of the stack's anchors
# (capsule 0.35, tank 1.0, engine 0.15 -- the 0.57-0.60 this used to read), because the
# tank and engine sit in the pod's shadow and vote for nothing. Off-cruise rows are the
# kick and the first apex, where the same stack presents 0.85..0.92 m2 to a flow it is
# meeting at an angle: a separate ceiling, not the cruise band stretched until it tests
# nothing.
CHECK all(0.78 < d["a"] < 0.90 for d in drag if d.get("a") and abs(d["aoa"]) <= 6 and abs(d["beta"]) <= 6)
CHECK all(d["a"] < 1.00 for d in drag if d.get("a"))
# The CLAIM's Cd pair is pinned beside the area it weights: 0.373..0.429 on cruise rows,
# measured (0.565..0.601 while the whole stack voted). A bill is Cd x A, so pinning one
# without the other leaves half the claim untestable -- which is how this row stayed
# green through a 1.44x change in the air. The off-cruise rows read up to 0.463.
CHECK all(0.36 < d["cd"] < 0.45 for d in drag if abs(d["aoa"]) <= 6 and abs(d["beta"]) <= 6)
CHECK all(d["cd"] < 0.50 for d in drag)
# Wings at 0.010 profile: 0.464..0.687 m2 of profile bill on cruise rows, 0.928 at the
# first apex where the wing is working hardest at 45.6 m/s.
CHECK all(0.44 < d["profile"] < 0.72 for d in drag if d.get("profile") is not None and abs(d["aoa"]) <= 6 and abs(d["beta"]) <= 6)
CHECK all(d["profile"] < 1.00 for d in drag if d.get("profile") is not None)
# None-guarded because #560 made Induced= dashable: every row of this run prints a
# number, but max() over a dict comprehension that can yield None is a TypeError waiting
# for the first row that does not.
# The floor is the claim "the wing is billed at all": smallest induced bill of this run
# is 0.134 m2 (t=1 s, at 81 m/s where the wing is barely loaded). A ceiling with no
# floor stays green if the law stops charging -- an AR of zero short-circuits inducedCd
# in drag.h, and a pane that falls out of its lifting system is skipped by the bill --
# while every other band here (aoa, sm, profile, a) still passes. The cruise ceiling
# catches the other direction: a hangar-sized induced bill showing up as a wall in level
# flight. Worst row of the run is the kick at 0.652, where the rudder's own side force
# is being paid for.
CHECK all(d["induced"] > 0.10 for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None)
CHECK all(d["induced"] < 0.45 for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None and abs(d["aoa"]) <= 6 and abs(d["beta"]) <= 6)
CHECK all(d["induced"] < 0.75 for d in drag if d.get("induced") is not None)
# #694's ratio, on the rows the CLAIM's glide language rests on: 5.3..9.4 across the
# whole run and 8.3..8.8 whenever the hull is settled. A lift/drag band is the one place
# where the wing's and the hull's bills have to agree with each other, so it reddens when
# either side's authored number moves on its own.
CHECK all(4.5 < d["ld"] < 11.0 for d in drag if d.get("ld") is not None)
# What this pins is MAGNITUDE with the sign up for grabs, and that is a change of claim:
# the band used to be -0.20..-0.02 because the hull's push had to stay BEHIND the COM,
# "or the hull levers angle of attack instead of weathervaning it". #1171 took 122 kg out
# of the nose, the COM walked aft, and the pod's frontal disc crossed to +0.17 m -- the
# hull now levers alpha, and the bigger phugoid above is the consequence. #1203 is that
# claim; until it settles this pins only that the bill's lever stays near the COM
# (|lever| <= 0.25 m) and so never dominates the tail's weathervane (CpL above).
# Two lines of action cannot fold into one scalar without lying about at least one of
# them: the perpendicular push gets its own SideCp= beside this.
CHECK all(-0.25 < d["formcp"] < 0.25 for d in drag if d.get("formcp") is not None)
# #947, pinned on the airframe its body was written against. This hull flies a degree or
# two off its line, so most rows have crossflow; the hull's strip is half-shielded by the
# pod ahead of it and bills the rest; and the normal force lands aft, doing the
# weathervane work FormCp alone could not. Measured 0.10..0.47 m2 at -0.81..-1.03 m.
# The floor guards "the law bills at all"; the band around -0.95 guards the SIGN, which
# is this case's claim made checkable in its own column -- and it is the one lever that
# did NOT flip when the COM walked.
CHECK all(d["sidecda"] is not None and d["sidecda"] > 0.05 for d in drag)
CHECK all(-1.4 < d["sidecp"] < -0.75 for d in drag if d.get("sidecp") is not None)
# The cause of every move above, pinned where it can be seen rather than inferred.
CHECK any(m["ship"] == "skiff" and m["parts"] == 13 and abs(m["mass"] - 446.24) < 1.0 for m in mass)
