# #1097: the hull itself can LIFT. A body part's air forces used to be the axial union
# bill (anti-parallel to the flow by construction) plus, since #947, a crossflow normal
# force on only the fraction of its own shadow the union REFUSED. A fully exposed hull
# at angle of attack paid nothing perpendicular -- so a wingless lifting body could not
# fly and the Shuttle's belly trim did not exist. This case is the ticket's claim made
# playable: a 4 m flat plate with an authored body_lift (liftbody_4m in res/data/parts.json
# -- the first part in the catalog to opt in; every pre-existing def stays at 0), no wings,
# no tails, no pilot, spawned prograde into 8.5 km air and left completely alone.
#
# THE ANCHORS ARE A PAIR (#1107). body_lift and the three drag anchors were re-fitted
# against each other on 2026-11-23: cl 0.65, cdForward 0.1, cdSide 1.0 (was 0.9 / 0.1 /
# 1.1). At 90 deg the sin cos kernel is silent, so the whole broadside normal force is
# drag_cd x cdSide = 1.20 -- inside a real flat plate's 1.17-1.28, where the old 1.32 sat
# outside -- and the pair's best glide, cl / (2 sqrt(cdSide x cdForward)), is 1.03, i.e.
# at the ~1:1 a plate can do rather than a third over it. tests/test_aerosweep.cpp sweeps
# the shipped def over alpha and pins those three claims; this file pins what the PAIR
# does in a real flight, which is where the ratio is read, not assumed.
#
# The hull is deliberately ONE part: the force acts at the part's PROJECTED
# CENTROID (#1106), and liftbody_4m's plate is point-symmetric, so that centroid
# IS its mount -- the one bolt, which is the pivot: the arm is zero and there is
# no pitch moment at all. Attitude stays exactly where the bed put it
# and the angle of attack grows only as gravity bends the flight path under it. That
# makes the run readable: AoA is 24.9 deg at the first logged row and climbs as gravity
# steepens the path beneath the plate, and the whole story is the vertical force balance.
#
# THE RUN'S SHAPE (measured, byte-identical over four concurrent copies -- a free hull
# has no contact solver to split on): 800 scripted ticks at time-accel 4 print 13 [drag]
# rows 4 sim-seconds apart, t=4 s to t=52 s. At t=4 the hull flies 24.9 deg AoA with
# SideCdA 0.00 / BodyCdA +1.35: the #947 law acts on this hull NOTHING (every part
# exposed, refused floored to zero) while the #1097 law acts on everything it sees --
# the exposed half of the two laws' split, in the shipped log (the middle and hidden
# halves are pinned in test_aerosweep). |F| settles to ~1.2 kN and holds against the
# 1.26 kN weight (128 kg) for the rest of the fall; Ld stays positive throughout
# (0.96 -> 0.01 as the flow swings under the plate, BodyCdA -- a force meter now, q x
# it is the summed lift's size -- peaking +2.36 at t=12 then decaying to +0.10 as
# cos -> 0 at 88.9 deg), and the 48 s of flying lose 1.12 km of altitude where a
# ballistic drop would have lost ~11 km. With one part there is no restoring moment, so
# the plate never trims at a fixed AoA -- a hull that cruises level needs tails, and that
# balance is a bed of its own, not this law's job. Since #1106 the same rows also print a
# CLa=/SM= pair for a wingless hull (CLa>0 at first, SM +0.00 neutral here); the pair's
# numbers are pinned by the hull-only benches in test_aerosweep, not re-pinned on this bed.
NAME wingless-lifting-body
CLAIM A flat plate with an authored body_lift and no lifting surfaces flies: BodyCdA > 0 on every row while SideCdA stays 0.00 (the exposed half of the #947/#1097 split) and the surface-lift column |L| stays 0, Ld > 0 and never better than a flat plate's, and the sink is bounded far short of ballistic.
KIND golden
ARGS --startship liftbody,res/ships/liftbody.json,Kerbin,flying-cruise
ARGS --drag-log --time-accel 4 --sim-steps 800 --timeout 300 --no-paint
LIMIT 90
EXPECT [drag]
FORBID error:
CHECK len(drag) >= 10
# THE BILL EXISTS, at a scale that is not rounding. BodyCdA folds the sin in (q x it
# is the summed body-lift force's SIZE): the plate's silhouette on the row (A=) is
# 4.5..6.7 m2 and body_lift 0.65 x drag_cd 1.2 x sin x cos puts it at +1.35 on the
# first row, peaking +2.36 at t=12 and decaying to +0.10 at t=52, where the near-
# vertical fall (AoA 88.9) drives cos -> 0. 2.0 on the peak keeps the mid-arc story
# with 15% to spare; 0.05 on the tail is the print quantum with a factor of two, which
# is all a decaying column can honestly promise.
CHECK all(d["bodycda"] >= 0.05 for d in drag if d.get("bodycda") is not None)
CHECK max(d["bodycda"] for d in drag if d.get("bodycda") is not None) >= 2.0
# THE PARTITION, ITS EXPOSED HALF: a fully exposed hull is refused NOTHING by the
# axial union, so the crossflow law keeps its hands off while the body-lift law acts
# on everything it sees. Pinned exactly on 0.00: this hull is one part, so "exposed"
# is not a near-miss. (The middle and the hidden half of the split belong to
# test_aerosweep -- a one-part hull's log cannot speak for both laws.)
CHECK all(d["sidecda"] == 0.0 for d in drag if d.get("sidecda") is not None)
# No lifting SURFACE is aboard: |L| stays 0.00 on every row while Ld is positive --
# the perpendicular force this hull flies on is the hull's own, and it shows up only
# in Ld='s split of the air's force, never in a wing's lift sum.
CHECK all(d["L"] == 0.0 for d in drag)
# The force is perpendicular to the flow, so it reads as Ld>0 with no wings aboard
# (the |L| column is SURFACE lift and stays 0 -- Ld= is the air's split of the total
# force, which is exactly the point). Measured 0.96 at t=4, still >= 0.9 at t=8.
CHECK all(d["ld"] > 0.0 for d in drag if d.get("ld") is not None)
CHECK any(d["ld"] >= 0.9 for d in drag if d.get("ld") is not None and d["t"] <= 8.0)
# THE PAIR'S CEILING, from the flight rather than the bench: the closed form
# cl / (2 sqrt(cdSide x cdForward)) = 1.03 at 17.5 deg, and no row of a real arc beats
# it (measured max 0.96). A hull that ever logged Ld above ~1.05 with no wing aboard
# would be gliding better than a flat plate, which is the #1107 failure mode.
CHECK max(d["ld"] for d in drag if d.get("ld") is not None) <= 1.05
# ONE LINE OF ARITHMETIC, not two laws: Ld x (drag_cd x Cd x A) == BodyCdA on every
# row that prints enough digits (1.2 is the default bill multiplier; 5% is the print
# quantum -- the worst measured is 2.7%, on the row where Ld rounds from 0.13). The
# lift column and the drag column are the same pair of anchors seen from two sides.
CHECK all(abs(d["ld"] * 1.2 * d["cd"] * d["a"] - d["bodycda"]) <= 0.05 * d["bodycda"] for d in drag if d.get("ld") is not None and d["ld"] >= 0.1 and d.get("a") is not None)
# THE BROADSIDE ANCHOR, read off the arc: the hull weathervanes toward broadside
# (AoA 24.9 -> 88.9), so the axial Cd climbs to cdSide = 1.0 and cannot pass it --
# the blend's max over alpha IS cdSide for this def. Under the old 1.1 the top row
# printed 1.091; a ceiling here is what makes the refit visible in flight.
CHECK all(d["cd"] <= 1.0005 for d in drag)
CHECK max(d["cd"] for d in drag) >= 0.9995
# ONE-PART HULL: the lift lands on the projected centroid, and this plate's
# centroid IS the mount, and the mount IS the pivot -- the lever reads zero
# honestly rather than hiding a fabricated arm (cf. #947's review, where a
# noise ratio printed as a plausible +0.52 m arm). #1106 is what makes this pin
# mean something: the arm is now real geometry that happens to be zero, not a
# law that cannot see one.
CHECK all(d["bodycp"] == 0.0 for d in drag if d.get("bodycp") is not None)
# THE GLIDE, bounded: 48 s of flying lose 1.12 km (measured); a ballistic fall from the
# same bed would lose ~11.3 km. The 3 km floor is that gap with room for the transient.
CHECK drag[-1]["alt_asl"] - drag[0]["alt_asl"] > -3000.0
