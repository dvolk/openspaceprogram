# THE WHEELS OF A GEARED HULL, SAME QUESTION AS 1158-a (#1158). A plane on the runway looked
# like it sat on invisible tyres: gear.obj draws a wheel whose bottom is 0.35 m below the part
# origin (matching wheel_radius = 0.35, so the DRAWN wheel is right), yet the hull rested ~0.3
# m higher than that. The gap was wheel_travel = 0.4 -- the contact face rides at the drawn
# radius and the strut holds the hull up by the unused part of its stroke, so the visible
# float is travel - W/(n*k) exactly (src/wheel.h "RIDE HEIGHT").
#
# The runway hull is where the trade-off bites, because a gear spring is soft (25000 N/m
# against a leg's 60000) and the tyres carry the whole 658.11 kg skiff. At 0.15 the loaded
# main compresses 0.106 m of its 0.15, so this hull has 1.4x of load left before that wheel
# reaches its bump stop -- under the even three-way share (n*k*travel/g = 1.15 t), because
# the three wheels carry 41/29/29 % of the hull. They did not when this case was written: on
# the 658.65 kg pre-#1171 hull the main took 42% on a third of the wheels and the ceiling was
# only ~0.91 t; #1171 took 122 kg out and #1204's pod allowance put it back, which is why
# the share reads 41% again. #1181 is the record of that mass change moving the float column;
# the mass is pinned below so the next one says so here instead.
#
# 850-runway-rest already pins that the hull does not sink or slide here; this pins where the
# drawn rubber meets the deck.
NAME gear-stands-close-on-the-runway
CLAIM A geared hull's drawn tyres touch the runway within 0.1 m, with suspension stroke left over.
KIND golden
ARGS --time-accel 1 --sim-steps 400 --startship skiffgear,res/ships/skiff_gear.json,Kerbin,pad,site=ksc_runway
ARGS --leg-log --mass-log --no-paint --orbit-interval 0.5
LIMIT 20
EXPECT [leglog]
FORBID error:
CHECK len(leglog) >= 12
# The mass the bands below are derived from (see 1158-a for why this is pinned).
CHECK len(mass) >= 4
CHECK all(m["ship"] == "skiffgear" and abs(m["mass"] - 658.11) < 1.0 for m in mass)
# Three wheels (two mains + nose gear), all of them on the deck.
CHECK all(r["kind"] == "gear" and r["gap"] is not None for r in leglog if r["t"] > 1.0)
# travel - W/(n*k) = 0.150 - 658.11*9.81/75000 = 0.064 m on an even share, and the least
# loaded tyre gives the worst gap. Measured 0.041..0.077 m; at travel 0.4 this hull used to
# read 0.29..0.33. The band is looser than the legs' because the gear spring is soft and the
# hull is light, not because the measurement is softer.
CHECK max(r["gap"] for r in leglog if r["t"] > 1.0) < 0.1
# The drawn bottom is the drawn tyre: gear.obj spans z -0.35..0.65, wheel_radius = 0.35. This
# is what ties the gap column to the mesh rather than to the part origin.
CHECK all(abs(r["mesh"] - 0.35) < 0.005 for r in leglog)
CHECK all(abs(r["gap"] + r["comp"] - r["travel"]) < 0.01 for r in leglog if r["comp"] > 0)
# Loaded (0.073..0.109 m in) and not at the bump stop -- the load-headroom guard, see 1158-a.
# The heaviest corner (uid 22, 41% of the hull) has 0.044 m of its 0.15 stroke left. The guard
# reserves 0.02 m of that, so 0.024 m is what this case can lose: it reddens at comp 0.13, i.e.
# 0.13*25000/(9.81*0.41) = 808 kg of hull, about +150 kg. Bottoming the tyre is another ~275 kg
# past that. On the 536 kg hull this line sat 0.036 m from the reserve's edge instead.
CHECK all(0.03 < r["comp"] < r["travel"] - 0.02 for r in leglog if r["t"] > 1.0)
