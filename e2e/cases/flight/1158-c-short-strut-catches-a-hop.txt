# THE OTHER HALF OF SHORTENING THE STRUT (#1158). 1158-a and 1158-b buy a smaller visible
# float by cutting leg_travel from 0.4 to 0.15, and the same number is the whole landing
# stroke: compression is clamped to travel. This case lands on the shortened strut hard enough
# to find out what that costs, because "it stands lower" is worthless if the hull then bottoms
# out and tips.
#
# Stage after 0.5 s, throttle for 500 ms (T; the keys are SIM-clocked under --sim-steps,
# #472): the racer_legs hull reaches alt 11.9 m and comes back at 6.38 m/s (t=2.5). It does
# not tip -- worst nose.z 0.9897 through the rebound -- and it settles (|w| under 0.032
# after t=5, 0.0000 by the end, |v| 0.00 at the last row). The honest cost, which this case
# does not paper over: peak compression is 0.138 of the 0.15 stroke, so a 6 m/s arrival uses
# 92% of the landing stroke and leaves 12 mm before a leg is solid on its stop.
#
# The burn is 500 ms, not the 600 ms this case was written with, because the PREMISE is a
# 6 m/s arrival and the hull got lighter under it: #1171 began weighing parts off their own
# mesh and racer_legs went 2926.63 -> 2270.09 kg (#1181). At 600 ms the same ship now leaves
# the pad and arrives at 9.03 m/s (a 5.2 m climb, apex 13.6 m), and that result is worth
# recording even though it is not this case's: the hull dips to nose.z 0.9732 and spins up to
# |w| 0.166 rad/s, still rocking at t=5.4, settling only by t=7. Same strut, same legs,
# 3 m/s more. Shortening the burn keeps this case testing what its CLAIM says, instead of
# loosening the bands around a different experiment.
#
# At travel 0.2 the 6 m/s arrival used to peak at 0.186 of 0.20 -- nearly spent too -- while
# the hull kept bouncing: |w| over the t > 5 rows reached 0.074 instead of 0.032, which is
# what reddened this case at the old travel. That comparison was measured on the heavier hull
# and has not been re-run since #1171, so treat it as history rather than a pin. This is a
# tuning pin, not an invariant: it pins the stroke that leaves the hull rock-free after a hop.
#
# And stroke length is NOT what saves a genuinely bad landing: measured arrivals of 15.7 m/s
# tip the hull (nose.z goes to -0.35..-0.42, |w| peaks 1.5..2.0 rad/s) at travel 0.20, 0.15 and
# 0.13 alike -- again on the pre-#1171 hull, and the 9 m/s row above points the same way on the
# current one. What a landing needs is a strut that stays spring-borne, not a longer one --
# hence #1166, which would take the float out of the picture entirely.
NAME short-strut-catches-a-hop
CLAIM A legged hull that hops off its pads and comes down at 6 m/s does not reach its bump stops, tip, or keep bouncing.
KIND golden
ARGS --time-accel 1 --sim-steps 1200 --startship rl,res/ships/racer_legs.json,Kerbin,pad
ARGS --leg-log --dbg-log --att-log --mass-log --no-paint --orbit-interval 0.2
ARGS --sim-press 500,0,SPACE,700,500,T
LIMIT 40
EXPECT [leglog]
EXPECT [attlog]
FORBID error:
# The mass the 500 ms burn was chosen for. The arrival speed IS a function of hull mass, so
# a retune (#1171, which is what moved it 2926.63 -> 2270.09 kg) has to re-pick the burn
# length; this line is what says so instead of leaving the hop silently harder (#1181).
CHECK len(mass) >= 4
CHECK abs(mass[0]["mass"] - 2270.09) < 0.5
# The burn spends 5.7 kg of hydrazine, so the later rows are lighter by that much -- the pin
# is on the spawn mass, which is what the 500 ms burn was chosen against.
CHECK mass[-1]["mass"] < mass[0]["mass"]
# It really flew: 3.5 m of climb over the run (spawn rest 8.4 m), and a fast arrival.
CHECK max(d["alt"] for d in dbg) > 11.0
CHECK max(d["v"] for d in dbg) > 5.0
# The legs left the ground during the hop. gap is the drawn footpad's distance to the terrain
# under the hull, and on a planet groundContact's analytic fall-through answers however far
# away it is (8.8 m at the top of this hop), so a large gap IS the airborne row.
CHECK max(r["gap"] for r in leglog if r["gap"] is not None) > 1.0
# The clamp never engaged: compression stayed under travel (peak 0.138 of 0.15), so the spring
# was holding the hull rather than the leg sitting solid on its stop.
CHECK all(r["comp"] < r["travel"] for r in leglog)
# ... and the two columns are the real thing, not a degenerate read: the mesh pin is 1158-a's,
# repeated here because this case is the one that asserts a comp MAGNITUDE, and a logger whose
# mesh read collapsed to 0 would report comp ~0.07 and pass a bare `comp < travel`.
CHECK all(abs(r["mesh"] - 0.05) < 0.005 for r in leglog)
CHECK max(r["comp"] for r in leglog) > 0.05
CHECK all(abs(r["gap"] + r["comp"] - r["travel"]) < 0.01 for r in leglog if r["comp"] > 0)
# Upright the whole way, including the rebound row (worst 0.9897).
CHECK all(a["nose"][2] > 0.98 for a in att)
# And it settles: no residual rock after the bounce, and standing still at the end.
CHECK all(a["wnorm"] < 0.05 for a in att if a["t"] > 5.0)
CHECK all(a["wnorm"] < 0.01 for a in att[-5:])
CHECK abs(dbg[-1]["v"]) < 0.5
