# A hull standing on the ground now stands in a moving sky, and this is the case that asks
# what that does (#718). #688 made the air move; #717 made it readable. Neither said anything
# about the regime underneath 3 m/s, where a hull is not flying at all -- and applyAeroForce
# has no grounded gate, so a parked hull has been reading a non-zero airspeed ever since the
# first body authored a wind row.
#
# The answer, measured on Earth's pad bed (the only launch site on a windy body in the shipped
# system -- Venus has no pad, which is why nothing caught this):
#
#   wind at the datum   hs after 12 s      sit
#   none                8e-9 m/s           landed
#   5.45 m/s (shipped)  0.0025 m/s         landed
#   12 m/s              0.0012 m/s         landed
#   20 m/s              0.013 m/s          landed
#   30 m/s              3.09 m/s           FLYING LOW -- it is being dragged
#   60 m/s              0.37 m/s, at 1.93 m AGL instead of 5.70  (pushed over and along)
#
# So: the wind DOES push a grounded hull -- the residual ground speed goes up by three orders
# of magnitude between the windless control and the shipped row -- and the ground holds it. The
# threshold on this hull, in sea-level air, is between 20 and 30 m/s, and past it the hull
# stops being landed at all: at 30 m/s its ground speed crosses kShipGroundSpeed (3 m/s) and
# the situation flips to "flying low" while it is still sat on the plain.
#
# "Holds it" needs its shape, because a 12 s endpoint on its own is a number with no behaviour
# behind it. Run out to 32 s at the shipped row, the hull CREEPS and wobbles at the
# millimetre-per-second scale rather than settling to zero: hs reads 0.0012 at 6.8 s, rises
# through 0.0022 at 12.9 s, peaks near 0.004 around 17 s and is back to 0.00098 at 31.1 s,
# while roll takes up ~2.7 deg and alt_agl does not move (5.7040 -> 5.7035). That is a hull
# being pushed against the ground and losing by millimetres, not one sliding away. Worth
# writing down: extrapolating the 6.8..12.9 s rise alone would put it over 0.01 m/s by ~28 s,
# and the 32 s run says that rise is the first half of a wobble, not a slope.
#
# Why that matters for Venus. The tallest Venus ground reads ~4 m/s of air in a ~57 kg/m3
# column: q ~460 Pa, against Earth sea level's 0.5 x 1.225 x 30^2 = 551 Pa. The Venus highland
# regime is therefore on the DRAGGED side of that line, not the parked side. The shipped
# surface row (1 m/s in 65 kg/m3, q = 32 Pa) is harmless; it is the interpolated line between
# the two authored rows that is not. Nothing in the shipped game can show it yet, because there
# is no launch site on Venus to put a hull on -- which is its own finding, and the follow-up.
#
# What is pinned here is the shipped half: at the wind the files actually author, a landed hull
# stays landed and stays where it was put, while reading the air's speed as its own airspeed.
# That last part is by design and is the point of #712's drifting platform -- a parked hull in
# moving air HAS a non-zero airspeed -- so the assertion is not "tas is zero", it is "tas is the
# wind and hs is not".
NAME a-hull-on-the-ground-stands-in-a-wind
CLAIM A hull landed at an Earth pad stands in 5.4 m/s of moving air without leaving its place: tas reads the wind, hs stays under 0.01 m/s, and it stays landed for the whole run.
KIND golden
ARGS --timeout 12 --system res/systems/solar_system.json --info-log --startship v,res/ships/skiff_trim.json,Earth,pad
FORBID error:
FORBID place_vehicle
# The pad bed needs a launch site, and Earth is the only windy body with one. If that ever
# stops being true the spawn says so out loud and this FORBID fires, rather than the hull
# landing somewhere else and the case quietly pinning a different world.
# The air is moving over a hull that is not: tas= is |airRelativeVel|, the speed the aero law
# bills, and it sits on the datum row interpolated at this pad's 140.7 m ASL -- 5 + (140.7 /
# 11000) x 35 = 5.448 m/s, not zero and not the hull's own speed. wind_east= is the table's
# answer for the same place, so the two agree by construction (and read 5.4477 / 5.4465 in the
# log, tas a hair under the wind because the hull is creeping downwind of it).
CHECK all(s["tas"] is not None and 5.3 < s["tas"] < 5.6 for s in surf if s["t"] > 6.0)
CHECK all(abs(s["wind_east"] - 5.448) < 0.01 and s["wind_north"] == 0.0 for s in surf if s["t"] > 6.0)
# And the hull does not go with it. Measured 0.0025 m/s at t=11.9s against 8e-9 with the wind
# rows dropped from the file (tmp/earth_nowind.json, not shipped): the push is real and the
# ground holds it. 0.01 m/s is far above the residual and far below anything that reads as
# creep, so a hull that started sliding breaks this rather than tuning it. The bound is not a
# timeout artifact -- the header's 32 s run holds hs under 0.004 the whole way, and this case
# stops at 12 s because it is a behaviour pin, not a patience test.
CHECK all(s["hs"] < 0.01 for s in surf if s["t"] > 6.0)
# Still on the ground, in the sim's own words, for the whole pinned window. This is the
# assertion the 30 m/s experiment breaks: at that speed the same hull reads 3.09 m/s of ground
# speed and sit="flying low" while still sat on the plain.
CHECK all(s["sit"] == "landed" for s in surf if s["t"] > 6.0)
# It did not get pushed OVER either: alt_agl converges to 5.70 m and holds to a millimetre.
# That is the observable the 60 m/s experiment breaks -- there the same hull ends up lying at
# 1.93 m AGL -- so it is pinning the wind's effect, not the terrain. The pad spawn does tip the
# hull in the first ~4 s (pitch 86 deg -> ~2.9 deg), which is the spawn settling and not the
# wind: the windless control tips the same way. Hence the pin starting at 8 s.
CHECK all(abs(s["alt_agl"] - 5.70) < 0.2 for s in surf if s["t"] > 8.0)
