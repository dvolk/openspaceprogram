NAME runway-brake-at-10x
CLAIM A gear hull that rolls up under thrust at 10x warp brakes to a stop and stays stopped -- and at the speed it reaches, its own wings lift three quarters of its weight off the tires, which is what the brake budget turns out to be short by.
KIND invariant
ARGS --time-accel 10 --startship skiffgear,res/ships/skiff_gear.json,Kerbin,pad,site=ksc_runway
ARGS --sim-steps 300 --dbg-log --orbit-interval 0.5 --drag-log --mass-log
# Brakes are on at spawn: release (O), roll up under thrust, then brake (O) again.
# --sim-steps so the press schedule counts logic ticks, not the wall (#472): 1000 ms
# is 60 ticks whatever the accel, and at warp 10 that is 10 s of sim.
ARGS --sim-press 500,0,O
ARGS --sim-press 1000,700,T
ARGS --sim-press 2000,0,O
EXPECT [dbg]
# Why this case exists at warp 10 when 852 does the same story at warp 1: the substep
# here is 3 x 0.0556 s, the coarsest the shipped loop makes (warp >= 11 rides rails),
# and that is the only regime in which wheel.h's momentum clamp can bind (#770). Every
# other driving case ran at warp 1, where the criterion 2.7*g*h is ~0.04 and the clamp
# is provably dead code -- so the whole warp 8-10 rolling regime had no net under it.
#
# What it measures is that the clamp did not eat the rolling law. That is still the job;
# what the flight looks like while doing it is not what this file used to say.
# WHAT THE AERO RUN DID TO THIS ROLL (#1132 family D, re-measured 2026_11_23). The hull
# weighs 658.65 kg x Kerbin's 9.8112 m/s2 = 6462 N -- the [mass] row prints the 658.65, so
# the pin below is the game's own number and not a hand calculation. Skiffgear carries two
# 4 m wings, a tail, an elevator pair, ailerons and a rudder, and once it is rolling the air
# bills them: L_up = +4961.4 N world-up at 40.0 m/s, +4650.0 N at 44.4 m/s, three quarters of
# the hull's weight taken off the tires. Braking friction is proportional to what is left, so
# the brake press at t=20 s no longer finishes inside 5 s: measured 28.2-30.6 m/s at t=25 and
# 0.07-6.0 at t=30 across six copies of this schedule, near-rest from t=35 apart from the pops
# described under REST -- where the old file recorded 0.47 m/s at t=25 and 0.01 from t=30.
# The hull never leaves the ground (alt peaks at 2.3 m against the 2.2 m it sits at on gear),
# so this is a light hull on the runway, not a flying one, and the clamp story stands.
# IT ARRIVES UPSIDE DOWN -- FIVE TIMES IN SIX. Up= walks 1.00 (t=15, 20) -> 0.88 (t=25) ->
# -0.61 (t=30) -> -1.00 (t=35 on) in four copies of this schedule; one stopped at -0.84; one
# never tipped at all (min Up 0.93, and its aero pitch moment peaked at 3644 Nm instead of the
# usual 10774). The aero torque -- |tau| up to 10774 Nm against a 100 Nm pod -- is what turns
# the hull, because the sim never touches a control key: SAS is off and the reaction wheels
# idle. Recorded in #1136. It is NOT pinned here, deliberately: the six copies press their keys
# on logic ticks (--sim-steps 300), so they are supposed to be identical, and they still split
# into a tipped and an untipped outcome -- the same load-dependent contact state as #953 below.
# A CHECK on the flip would be a coin flip; the L_up pin is not, measuring 4961.4 N in all six.
# The --drag-log and --mass-log lines this case now reads are output: the [dbg] rows with
# them are byte-identical to the same schedule without them (measured side by side), so the
# new pins do not buy their behaviour with extra sampling.
CHECK max(d["v"] for d in dbg) > 30
CHECK max(d["alt"] for d in dbg) < 5.0
# Three quarters of the weight is the fact; 0.7 leaves room for the roll to change a little
# and is still nowhere near "the wings did nothing" (measured 4961.4 N at 40.0 m/s against a
# 4523.4 N threshold, and L_up is 4961.4 in every one of six copies run).
CHECK max(d["L_up"] for d in drag if d["v"] > 35) > 0.7 * mass[0]["mass"] * 9.811
# REST, and the honest size of it. The two pins this block replaces both assumed a hull that
# parks and stays parked, and both were green only by luck:
#   * Travel measured from ds[-5], five log steps before the end. That row used to be stopped;
#     today the hull is still rolling at 6.00 m/s at t=30, so the pin measured braking distance
#     (4.0 m) rather than rest -- deterministically red, and fixed by anchoring on the window's
#     own first row. The "< 3.0" then read as 3 SQUARE metres, i.e. a 1.73 m window, which is
#     what it is compared against below; it is written 4.0*4.0 so the unit is metres.
#   * |v| < 0.1 on the LAST row. Six copies of this schedule, run standalone, all near-rest by
#     t=35 (one at 0.58 on that row) and still disagree about the ending. Last four rows (t=35,
#     40, 45, 50 -- 15 s of sim, not the 25 s this file used to claim), |v| per row, and travel
#     from the window's own first row:
#         0.01 0.01 0.01 1.39   2.1 m      0.04 0.04 0.04 0.04   0.6 m
#         0.01 0.01 0.01 0.18   2.4 m      0.01 0.01 0.01 0.01   0.2 m
#         0.03 0.06 0.15 0.05   0.4 m      0.58 0.03 0.03 0.02   1.1 m
#     The hull sometimes POPs: alt walks 0.3 -> 2.3 m with |v| 1.39 sitting on the exact row the
#     old pin read, so two of those six would have failed it. The rest depth varies with it
#     (0.3 m in one copy, 2.1-2.3 m in another, against the 2.2 m the hull stands at on gear at
#     spawn), which is the suspension and not the runway. That is #953, commented with this
#     table on 2026_11_23: the step is fixed (--physics-hz, game.h:867), so nothing here should
#     care how busy the box is, and something does.
# A count pin ("3 of 4 rows under 0.2") was tried first and is no better: it scored exactly 3 of
# 4 in two of the six, so it had no margin left either. And a 2 m/s ceiling, written from those
# six, is ALSO still a coin flip -- see the control below. What is pinned is a 4 m window and a
# 4 m/s ceiling across the window, against measured worsts of 2.4 m and 2.72 m/s. Deliberately
# loose -- the defect is filed, not hidden -- and it still bites: without the brake press the
# hull is still rolling at 6.73 m/s at t=50 and a hundred metres down the runway (MUTATION).
CHECK (lambda ds: max(sum((b["pos"][j]-ds[-4]["pos"][j])**2 for j in (0,1,2)) for b in ds[-4:]) < 4.0*4.0)(dbg)
CHECK max(abs(d["v"]) for d in dbg[-4:]) < 4.0
# THE CONTROL THAT MAKES THIS A BUG AND NOT NOISE (2026_11_23, four and four on one binary, the
# full press schedule above). Run one after another, the hull parks at alt 0.3 m and every log is
# byte-identical -- same rows t=35..50, |v| 0.01, travel 0.2 m. Launch four at once and they are
# identical right up to t=35, then split on the very next row:
#     copy A  t=40 alt 1.4 m |v| 0.02      copy B  same as A
#     copy C  t=40 alt 2.5 m |v| 0.66     copy D  t=40 alt 1.8 m |v| 2.72 m/s
# All four settle again by t=45. An 8-way launch does the same thing: three copies stay parked at
# 0.3 m (travel 0.2 m), four pop to 1.4-1.5 m, and two are kicked hard -- |v| 0.15 and 2.83 m/s
# on a row that every idle-box run prints as 0.01. Across the 22 copies of this schedule measured
# on this build the worsts are |v| 2.83 m/s and 2.40 m of travel inside the window. So the run is
# reproducible when the box is idle and not when it is busy, at a fixed dt (game.h:867) with
# tick-timed input -- a 2.8 m/s kick and 2.2 m of rise out of nowhere, which is why the two
# numbers above are envelopes and not laws. #953 has this table; tightening them back is the proof
# that it is fixed.
# MUTATION, measured: drop the second O press and the hull is still rolling at 6.73 m/s at
# t=50 (and 20.98 at t=35), so the rest pins above are the brakes' doing and not the air's.
