# An autopilot armed on Venus's cloud deck must size its turn from the air ABOVE the
# deck, not from the whole sky the body happens to have (#962). The deck holds 56 km
# of a 116 km air (res/data/venus_launch_sites.json, res/systems/solar_system.json),
# so the hull starts with 60 km to climb and the ~47 kPa still over it -- about
# 5300 kg/m^2 in Venus's 8.87 m/s^2 -- to push back. Credited the body, the law saw
# 116 km and 1.04e6 kg/m^2 and scheduled a 40 km turn around a ship that was born
# 56 km inside its own sky.
#
# This case exists for the GLUE: airAboveHull() reads the two numbers off a hull, a
# body and a building bubble frame, none of which tests/test_guidance.cpp can reach
# (the law is pure). The arm line prints what the law holds (#961), so a driver that
# measured from the datum, or from |com| inside a bubble (#850), fails here.
#
# The numbers are bounded, not exact: the deck's own altitude is wherever the balloon
# happens to be (56,004 m in the run this was written from) and the pressure comes off
# the profile at that height.
NAME deck-credits-only-the-air-above-it
CLAIM An autopilot armed on an air platform schedules against the air above the deck, not the body's whole atmosphere.
KIND invariant
ARGS --system res/systems/solar_system.json --startship racer,res/ships/racer.json,Venus,pad,site=venus_cloud_platform --ascent 100e3 --time-accel 10 --sim-steps 900 --no-paint --ascent-log
LIMIT 60
EXPECT Autopilot: ascent armed on 'racer'
CHECK float(re.search(r"([\d.]+) m of air overhead", out).group(1)) > 58e3
CHECK float(re.search(r"([\d.]+) m of air overhead", out).group(1)) < 62e3
CHECK float(re.search(r"of air overhead, ([\d.]+) kg/m\^2", out).group(1)) > 4500
CHECK float(re.search(r"of air overhead, ([\d.]+) kg/m\^2", out).group(1)) < 6000
# The schedule the hull is flown on. The deck's own air -- 0.4 x min(60 km overhead,
# 100 km ask), weighted 5300/10000 -- sizes a 12.7 km turn, and since the turn is
# measured from where the launch begins (#979) the hull starts near vertical and is
# 45 deg over about 13 km up: the rows run 87.9, 74.2, 56.8, 39.3 as it climbs from
# 56.0 to 71.8 km. Two wrong readings are excluded by these bounds. Crediting the
# whole body sizes a 40 km turn, which at those same heights would still be asking
# 70+ deg; and measuring the turn from the DATUM (#979's bug) put a hull at 56 km
# fifty-six kilometres into its own 12.7 km turn, so its first tick asked 12.7 deg
# and #930's apex_alt_ ratchet kept it there -- the profile that sank below the deck.
# Flown rows only: after the verdict the mission keeps printing rows with the pitch
# frozen at the verdict tick's ask, and a Coast row prints pitch=0.0, so a walk over ALL
# rows measures what a dead law last said rather than what the profile asked.
CHECK max(a["pitch"] for a in ascent if a["phase"] == "ascent") > 80.0
CHECK max(a["pitch"] for a in ascent if a["phase"] == "ascent" and a["alt_asl"] > 68e3) < 50.0
CHECK min(a["pitch"] for a in ascent if a["phase"] == "ascent") > 30.0
# What this case does NOT pin is the mission's fate. It ends phase=failed at t=74.3 s
# with why="the tanks are dry", having climbed from 56.0 to 71.8 km with apoapsis
# 78.5 km, billed dv=1582.3 m/s of which grav 445.7, drag 429.8 and steer 165.9: a
# `racer` cannot buy 100 km on Venus out of 1.6 km/s. A hull-versus-body question, not
# an air-numbers one, and filed as #981. (When this case was written the run died at
# t=1.0 with why="the ship came back down" instead -- a spawn on a driven building had
# no collider in the world yet on the first tick, #977 -- so nothing was being flown at
# all and only the arm-time numbers could be checked.)
