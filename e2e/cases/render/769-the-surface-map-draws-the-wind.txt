# The Surface Map's fourth picture is the wind (#769). #745 built the seam and said so out
# loud -- "a wind vector field is the third in reach, so the seam goes in now" -- and #688 put
# real profiles in three bodies' files, with a Wind: row on the Surface window to read one
# number off them (#717). What was missing is the picture: which way the air moves, and where
# on this world it moves harder.
#
# Two halves, and the case pins both. The BASE is the speed, ramp-coloured over the span the
# GROUND can reach -- not the span the file happens to state, and not a global 0..500 either.
# Those are three different spans and the difference is the picture: Venus states 1 m/s at the
# datum and 100 m/s at 65 km, and its highest ground is 2.7 km, so a ramp taken from the rows
# paints that whole world inside the bottom 3% of the palette -- one colour, and a grid of
# arrows all the same length. So the sweep prices its own scale from the floor and ceiling it is
# about to colour, and the log prints both spans side by side (wind_ends= the file's, wind_ramp=
# the picture's) so the gap is visible instead of assumed. Under each pixel the altitude is that
# pixel's GROUND elevation above the sea-level datum, which is the same coordinate windAt() keys
# on and the same one the Surface window's Wind: row asks with, so a mountain pokes into faster
# air and shows up brighter. The ARROWS are the vector half: a speed field alone says where the
# air moves harder and nothing about which way. They are counted in the log as wind_cells=
# because a picture that paints its colours and forgets its ink looks exactly like one that did
# not.
#
# The refusals are the other half of the design, and they are the reason this is a case and not
# just a shader. A body with no air and a body whose file states a still sky are different news
# (#726), so each prints wind=- with a note naming which one, and neither falls back to the
# albedo -- that would put a ground picture under a "wind" label, which is the map lying about
# what it is (#727). Earth, the Moon and Pluto come from ONE run: the Body combo picks them, and
# a body pick re-bakes by itself, which is also what pins the pick reaching the sweep rather
# than only the label.
#
# Pluto is the second refusal because it is the last air in the shipped catalogue with no wind
# authored: #772 put rows on Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Titan, and their maps -- bands,
# belts, the row plan that drew them -- are pinned by render/772-a-real-world-winds-its-bands.
# Pluto's 1.3 Pa of nitrogen is air the map can ask about and a table it cannot answer.
#
# Why the Moon needs --ui-scroll: the Body popup shows nine rows and the Moon is the eleventh
# body in the file. NOTCHES is negative to go down, and two is enough -- four scrolls past it
# and the click reports a miss, which is the failure mode worth knowing about here.
#
# #826: the cursor legs used to read the FIRST cursor row of a single dump taken at 3.2 s, and
# that went flaky the moment a second game was running beside this one -- 2 of 3 runs with
# --jobs 3, never alone. The dump was the problem, not the mouse. The window is open by then and
# its toolbar is on screen, but the opening bake's job has not been collected yet, so the window
# takes its "Mapping the surface ..." path: the log for a failing run shows Body/Refresh/Map/Level
# with Refresh, Map and Level marked disabled and NO ##smnav row, and the Earth [surfmap] line
# printing AFTER the dump. A window with no published picture draws no cursor row at all, so the
# row list held one entry -- the placeholder from the late dump -- and the legs indexed a dash.
# Two fixes, both about WHERE the read happens: the dump moved to 4.2 s and a second follows at
# 4.8 s, and the legs ask for the rows that carry a reading rather than for row 0 and row 1, so a
# bake that lands a beat late shifts WHICH dump answers, not whether one does. Size of the bet,
# measured across the 769 logs kept under tmp/e2e/runs: the sweep itself is 5-6 ms and the Earth
# row printed before the first dump in 28 of 33 recordings; the five late ones printed between
# the 3.2 s dump and the 4.0 s click, so the worker was 1.1-1.9 s behind the post. The first dump
# now has ~0.8 s of headroom over that worst case and the second ~2.8 s. The mouse is NOT part of
# the fix -- ImGui keeps its position, so the one motion at 2.6 s is still live at both dumps and
# a second would do nothing. (A click does park the pointer off-window; that is what makes the
# LATE dump read dashes, together with the Body popup covering the map.)
NAME the-surface-map-draws-the-wind
CLAIM --surfmap-map wind paints the air's speed and direction over the ground: Earth prints the speeds it actually drew between the ends its file states, the Moon refuses for want of air, Pluto refuses because its authored air has no wind rows, and the cursor reads the number under it.
KIND invariant
ARGS --width 1600 --height 900 --timeout 13 --system res/systems/solar_system.json
ARGS --startship v,res/ships/skiff_trim.json,Earth,pad
ARGS --surfmap-log --surfmap-n 128 --surfmap-map wind --surfmap-body Earth
ARGS --ui-click 2000,"Windows/Surface Map"
ARGS --sim-mouse 2600,0,700,420,0
ARGS --ui-list 4200
ARGS --ui-list 4800
ARGS --ui-click 5400,"Surface Map/Body"
ARGS --ui-scroll 5800,"##Combo_00",-2
ARGS --ui-click 6400,"##Combo_00/Moon"
ARGS --ui-click 7400,"Surface Map/Body"
ARGS --ui-scroll 7800,"##Combo_00",1
ARGS --ui-list 8200
ARGS --ui-click 8600,"##Combo_00/Pluto"
FORBID FAILED
FORBID error:
# Three bodies, one run, every row naming its own picture -- a stale buffer under a new label
# is the #745 failure this shape keeps catching.
CHECK len(surfmap) >= 3
CHECK all(r["map"] == "wind" for r in surfmap)
# Earth's own ends: 5 m/s at the datum and 40 m/s at the 11 km deck, exactly the two rows
# res/systems/solar_system.json authors (#688) -- not a global constant, and not one row
# painted verbatim everywhere either.
CHECK [r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Earth"][-1]["wind_ends"] == (5.0, 40.0)
CHECK [r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Earth"][-1]["wind_rows"] == 2
# What the sweep painted, and the span its colours run over. Both sit inside the file's ends,
# and they agree with EACH OTHER: the ground never stands at the 11 km deck row, so the picture
# scales itself to the ground instead. A ramp taken back from the rows moves wind_ramp= to
# (5.00, 40.00) and breaks this, which is the difference between a map and a swatch.
CHECK [r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Earth"][-1]["wind"][0] == 5.0
CHECK (lambda w, rp: 15.0 < w[2] < 40.0 and w[1] > w[0] and abs(rp[0] - w[0]) < 0.02 and abs(rp[1] - w[2]) < 0.02 and rp[1] < 30.0)([r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Earth"][-1]["wind"], [r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Earth"][-1]["wind_ramp"])
# The vector half. At --surfmap-n 128 the grid stride is 8, so 16 columns x 8 rows: every part
# of the map is sampled. A count of 0 is the bug this column exists for -- colours with no ink.
# (Earth is one whole-sphere band, so this is the GRID path: a table with latitude structure
# draws one row per band instead, which is band-centred by construction. surfmapArrowRows in
# src/surfmap.h is where that choice is pinned, and wind_rows_from= in the log says which drew.)
CHECK [r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Earth"][-1]["wind_cells"] == 128
# A wind map's base colours are RAMP colours, so the albedo columns dash rather than report a
# meaningless mean of the palette (#727).
CHECK (lambda r: r["albedo"] is None and r["shaded"] is None)([r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Earth"][-1])
# The two refusals, kept apart (#726): neither prints numbers, neither prints the other's
# reason, and neither falls back to a ground picture. The row-plan columns dash with the rest --
# a refusal has no arrow rows, so reporting a plan for it would be a number for a picture that
# was never drawn.
CHECK (lambda m: m["wind"] is None and m["wind_ends"] is None and m["wind_cells"] is None and m["wind_bands"] is None and m["wind_arrow_rows"] is None and m["wind_rows_from"] is None and "no air" in m["wind_note"])([r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Moon"][-1])
CHECK (lambda p, m: p["wind"] is None and "no wind" in p["wind_note"] and p["wind_note"] != m["wind_note"])([r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Pluto"][-1], [r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Moon"][-1])
# THE NUMBER UNDER THE CURSOR is that pixel's number. The mouse parks over 1.4 km of ground at
# lat +46 rather than over the sea, deliberately: over the ocean the datum row answers exactly
# 5.0 m/s, which is the bottom of the painted range, so a readout printing the datum row verbatim
# would pass there forever. The direction is NAMED in the same eight-word vocabulary the Surface
# window's Wind: row uses -- not an angle, so the two windows describe one wind. The label may
# also carry a trailing ` site: ...`, so the wind segment is searched for rather than anchored to
# the end of the line: a launch marker drifting under the cursor must not break what this pins.
# (#826: "the cursor row" is not one row across two dumps, and the first dump can arrive before
# the opening bake lands -- so these scan the cursor rows that carry a READING and require at
# least one. "Carry a reading" is the filter, not "mentions wind": the dashed forms the window
# prints for a rebake and for a refusal -- "wind - (rebaking)", "wind - (no air ...)" (gameui.cpp)
# -- both contain " wind " and would enter the set, and then the speed leg dies on a None match,
# which is the broken-harness-looking failure this case is supposed to stop producing. A run where
# the map never draws a number has no row in the set and leg one says so.)
CHECK (lambda live: len(live) >= 1 and all(re.search(r"wind [0-9.]+ m/s (east|north-east|north|north-west|west|south-west|south|south-east|calm)", r["label"]) is not None for r in live))([r for r in uilist if r["window"] == "Surface Map" and r["label"].startswith("cursor: lat") and re.search(r"wind [0-9.]+ m/s ", r["label"])])
CHECK (lambda live, w: live and all(w[0] < float(re.search(r"wind ([-+]?[0-9.]+) m/s", r["label"]).group(1)) < w[2] for r in live))([r for r in uilist if r["window"] == "Surface Map" and r["label"].startswith("cursor: lat") and re.search(r"wind [0-9.]+ m/s ", r["label"])], [r for r in surfmap if r["body"] == "Earth"][-1]["wind"])
# Being INSIDE the painted span is not enough to prove the cursor asked about the pixel: the file
# states 5 m/s at the datum and 40 m/s at the 11 km deck, the span this sweep painted runs to
# 22.97, and the ship's pad sits on ~0.5 km of ground, where the same profile answers ~6.5 m/s --
# strictly inside, so a readout wired to the hull's altitude passes the leg above. The cursor's
# pixel is 1.4 km, where it answers 9.6. This band is 0.94-1.9 km of ground, so it separates the
# two wirings instead of merely disliking the extremes.
CHECK (lambda live: live and all(8.0 < float(re.search(r"wind ([-+]?[0-9.]+) m/s", r["label"]).group(1)) < 11.0 for r in live))([r for r in uilist if r["window"] == "Surface Map" and r["label"].startswith("cursor: lat") and re.search(r"wind [0-9.]+ m/s ", r["label"])])
# And the cursor leaves the map when the mouse does -- here, when the Body click and the wheel
# scroll park the pointer off the window (the popup covering the map is a second, sufficient
# cause). Dashed, not a stale reading of the last map pixel. LAST rather than index 1, and
# stamped >= 7.0 s so the dashes must come from the late dump: two pre-popup dumps can put the
# placeholder anywhere in the list, and "the last row is a dash" alone would also be satisfied by
# a run where the late dump drew nothing at all.
CHECK (lambda cur: cur[-1]["t"] >= 7.0 and cur[-1]["label"] == "cursor: lat --  lon --  elev --")([r for r in uilist if r["window"] == "Surface Map" and r["label"].startswith("cursor:")])
