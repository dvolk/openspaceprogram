NAME atlas-prices-the-sunshine
CLAIM Every dossier in this file answers the sun -- the light at that body's orbit, the albedo the body authors, the temperature those two imply, the surface temperature the file authors, and the gap between them -- and the rows are ONE arithmetic rather than five independent printings: the black-body follows from the sunlight and albedo rows beside it, the gap follows from the black-body and the surface row, each body's sunlight follows from the star's own luminosity row and that body's semi-major axis, and a satellite whose page states the WRONG KIND of albedo says so beside the converted number.
KIND invariant
ARGS --system res/systems/solar_system.json --timeout 6 --atlas-dump 4000
# --atlas-dump MS builds the dossier rows through the same atlasDossier() the Science tab
# renders, so this pins what the player reads without a GUI (#385). solar_system rather than
# the shipped ksp_system because solar_system is the file whose albedos the CORPUS states
# (utils/make_solar_system.py takes them from the fact sheets), which is what makes its rows
# answers rather than analogies. The shipped file used to author none -- #735 and #749 have
# since put all seventeen of its bodies on the table, by analogy and by conversion, and
# science/735 pins that half.
# Each CHECK here slices one dossier block, the way render/630 and render/552 do.
EXPECT [atlas] Earth weight=
EXPECT [atlas] Sun weight=
# 1. THE BLACK-BODY ROW FOLLOWS FROM THE TWO ABOVE IT: T = ((1-A) S / 4 sigma)^(1/4), sigma
#    the same constant the loader derives the star's luminosity from (src/solar.h's
#    kStefanBoltzmann). Measured
#    closure across the file's nine albedo-bearing bodies is 0.035 K -- pure print rounding
#    (S at %.4g, T at %.1f) -- so the band is 0.05 K. A dossier billing its rows independently
#    would be #699 in a window instead of a log, and the cheapest way to do that -- dropping
#    the factor 4, or the (1-A) -- misses by tens of kelvin.
CHECK (lambda m: abs(float(m.group(3)) - (float(m.group(1)) * (1.0 - float(m.group(2))) / 4.0 / 5.670374419e-8) ** 0.25) < 0.05)(re.search(r"sunlight \(mean orbit\)\s+([\d.]+) W/m2\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   bond albedo\s+([\d.]+)\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   black-body temp\s+([\d.]+) K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)))
# 2. AND THE GAP FROM THE TWO BESIDE IT. Earth reads +32.9 K under 1361 W/m2 and an albedo of
#    0.294 -- the number the fact sheets imply and never state. Venus reads +510.4 K, Mercury
#    +0.5 K: on a bare rock the authored reading and the airless mean agree, and the distance
#    between them on a world with air is what an atmosphere does. Nothing in the sim MODELS a
#    greenhouse (#728 is parked on exactly that), so the row reports a difference and its
#    label names the two numbers it is the difference of.
CHECK (lambda m: abs(float(m.group(3)) - (float(m.group(2)) - float(m.group(1)))) < 0.05)(re.search(r"black-body temp\s+([\d.]+) K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   surface temp\s+([\d.]+) K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   greenhouse gap\s+([+-]?[\d.]+) K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)))
# 3. THE STAR'S OWN ROW CARRIES THE OTHER END OF THE LEDGER. Earth's sunlight has to follow
#    from the Sun's printed luminosity and Earth's printed semi-major axis -- S = L / 4 pi a^2
#    -- or the two dossiers are describing two different suns. Band 1%: both figures print to 4
#    significant figures, and a is the mean rail, which is what the row's label says it is.
#    Worth recording: the first cut of THIS check typed 4 pi^2 and came out pi times low against
#    the game, which is the one way this file has of saying the checker is not above suspicion --
#    it reddened, the code did not, and the relation is what shipped.
CHECK (lambda e, u: abs(float(re.search(r"sunlight \(mean orbit\)\s+([\d.]+)", e).group(1)) - float(re.search(r"luminosity\s+([\d.e+]+) W", u).group(1)) / (4.0 * 3.141592653589793 * (float(re.search(r"semi-major axis\s+([\d.]+) Gm", e).group(1)) * 1e9) ** 2)) < 14.0)(re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0), re.search(r"\[atlas\] Sun weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# 4. A STAR IS the black body: its dossier reports its photosphere and its luminosity, and
#    neither a sunlight row (nothing is above it) nor a black-body row of its own.
CHECK re.search(r"surface temp \(photosphere\)\s+[\d.]+ K\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   luminosity\s+[\d.e+]+ W\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   \[orbit\]", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Sun weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# 5. A SURFACE THAT ONLY JUST LEARNED TO ANSWER. The Moon now authors a Bond albedo (0.11,
#    moonfact.html's Bulk parameters -- #737 found that the corpus stated it and our parser
#    read it only for planets), so its dossier closes: the light at its PLANET's orbit, the
#    albedo, and the black-body those two imply. Its surface temp and the gap still dash,
#    because no satellite page states a ground temperature (#722). A moon's light is labelled
#    for the rail it was taken from -- its own distance to the sun swings over its month, so
#    no single figure for the moon itself is honest.
CHECK re.search(r"sunlight \(parent's orbit\)\s+[\d.]+ W/m2\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   bond albedo\s+0\.110\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   black-body temp\s+27[01]\.[0-9] K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   surface temp\s+-\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   greenhouse gap\s+-", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# 5b. AND A SATELLITE THAT COULD NOT ANSWER UNTIL THE CORPUS WAS ASKED TWICE. The satellite
#     pages state a VISUAL GEOMETRICAL albedo (Europa's row in utils/rss/html/joviansatfact.html:
#     0.68) and no Bond albedo, and (1-A)S/4 needs the Bond one -- #736 filed that as a gap
#     rather than guessing at it. #749 closed it with the corpus's own arithmetic: ten pages in
#     this same corpus state BOTH albedos, their implied phase integrals scatter from 0.48 to
#     1.47, and the median (0.681) is the conversion -- with its spread printed every run, so
#     nobody mistakes the product for a reading. Europa therefore answers now, and the dossier
#     says which kind of number it is answering with: a row that read 0.463 bare would be a
#     converted figure dressed as a measurement. Revert the conversion -- or drop the marker --
#     and this check reddens. (The "no albedo at all" dash is still a live thing in the catalogue
#     moons, and science/727 pins it on Himalia in solar_system_named.json; this core file no
#     longer contains one, which is what #735 and #749 were for.)
CHECK (lambda m: abs(float(m.group(3)) - (float(m.group(1)) * (1.0 - float(m.group(2))) / 4.0 / 5.670374419e-8) ** 0.25) < 0.05)(re.search(r"sunlight \(parent's orbit\)\s+([\d.]+) W/m2\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   bond albedo\s+([\d.]+)\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   black-body temp\s+([\d.]+) K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Europa weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)))
# 6. Half-authored is half-authored. Pluto authors an albedo (0.720, the file's second
#    brightest after Venus's 0.770) and no ground reading, so its implied 32.2 K prints while
#    its surface temp and the
#    gap dash. A dash here is a missing FILE ENTRY, never a 0.0 -- the #727 law, which is
#    sharp for albedo: an albedo of 0.0 is a real reading (a body that absorbs everything the
#    star sends, the hottest case available) and prints 0.000, while "-" means the file never
#    said. Pluto and Titan are the two bodies the generator leaves without a ground reading.
CHECK re.search(r"bond albedo\s+0\.720\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   black-body temp\s+32\.[0-9] K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   surface temp\s+-\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   greenhouse gap\s+-", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Pluto weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# 7. #738: A BANDED GIANT HAS NO GROUND, and the dossier is the one place that has to say so.
#    The corpus authors "Temperature at 1 bar" for Jupiter (165.0 K, and #689 notes that IS our
#    datum level), and the gap beside its airless mean (+55.1 K) is mostly internal heat --
#    Jupiter radiates more than it absorbs -- so the word "greenhouse" there is a mechanism
#    claim the data does not support. atlasDossier() branches on Surface::bands: the three rows
#    become temp at 1 bar / level - airless mean / level swing. Revert that branch to the rocky
#    labels and both lines below go red.
CHECK re.search(r"black-body temp\s+109\.9 K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   temp at 1 bar\s+165\.0 K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   level - airless mean\s+\+55\.1 K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   level swing\s+-", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Jupiter weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK not re.search(r"greenhouse gap|surface temp", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Jupiter weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
#    And --solar-log agrees rather than contradicting it: a hull in a giant's air prints its
#    light columns (an albedo and an airless mean are as meaningful for a giant as for a rock)
#    and DASHES the ground columns, because there is no surface under the hull to stand on.
