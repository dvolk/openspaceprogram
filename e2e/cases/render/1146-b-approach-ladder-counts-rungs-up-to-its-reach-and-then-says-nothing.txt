NAME approach-ladder-counts-rungs-up-to-its-reach-and-then-says-nothing
CLAIM Out along the bore the ladder highlights the rung at the port's own face gap and, past its 20 m of rungs, stops claiming a rung at all rather than sticking at the last one.
KIND invariant
# --dock-test stale-target parks dockprobe 1 m of arc behind dockstation with a 40 deg yaw,
# and the two orbits pull them apart along the bore at roughly 2.7 m per 2 s at the start, so
# one 24 s run walks the whole ladder and a little past it. --orbit-interval 2.0 prints
# [dockhud] every 2 s, which is about one rung per row.
#   The rate is NOT constant and that is geometry, not a regression (#1201, 2026_11_26):
#   faceout is the gap projected on the targeted port's axis, and that axis swings a long way
#   across these rows (world axis x -0.656 -> -0.257), so the projection flattens out at
#   ~22 m -- 2.945, 5.678, ... 19.479, 20.747, 21.608, 22.031, 22.012 -- where the case used
#   to see 2.555 -> 27.864 m. The ladder never notices: past count x step it prints near=0
#   whatever the pair does next. So the walk CHECK below asks for monotonic OUT TO past the
#   reach and for the run to END past the reach, instead of for a fixed 24 m terminal figure
#   that only the swing's tail could satisfy. (The fixture's own offsets moved a little too --
#   #1171 measures parts off their meshes.)
#   This is the case that gives the reach teeth. The numbers -- 2 m squares, 2 m apart, 10 of
#   them -- are tuning a designer will change, so they are not pinned here; what is pinned is
#   the RELATION between the printed distance and the printed highlight:
#     near == round(faceout / step) while faceout is inside count x step, and near == 0 past
#     it. 17.816 m reads rung 9, 20.747 m reads nothing.
#   A highlight that clamped -- which is what a naive implementation does -- keeps saying "the
#   10th rung, just ahead" from 30 m, 300 m, anywhere. It would look fine on screen at the
#   start of an approach and lie for the rest of the session, which is the failure mode this
#   row exists to catch: the ladder is a distance instrument, and an instrument that stops
#   resolving distance while still showing one is worse than no ladder.
#   The 13 verdicts' draw/no-draw split is pinned in tests/test_dock.cpp; 1146-c pins the
#   branch that a fixture CAN reach. Nothing here claims pixels -- see 1146-a for what a
#   [dockhud] row does and does not prove.
ARGS --dock-test stale-target --time-accel 1 --timeout 24 --dockhud-log --orbit-interval 2.0
EXPECT Dock-test (stale-target)
EXPECT [dockhud] t=
# The pair is refused for a GEOMETRIC reason the ladder is there to help fix, so it draws --
# and it keeps drawing as they drift, because the gate does not refuse on distance.
