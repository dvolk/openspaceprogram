CLAIM A world's dossier now carries its SWING -- the ground's coldest and hottest ends, the one knob that ties them, and the temperature they swing around -- and only for worlds whose source actually states a night and a day. Venus's page says its ground range is about zero, so its two ends print the same number and the knob says 1.000; Mercury's page says nothing about a swing, so its row dashes instead of showing the mean twice. The Moon's row says its anchor is DERIVED, because no page states a ground temperature there. And the two ends are not two free numbers: one knob forces both from the anchor, which is what makes this row arithmetic a test can check rather than two figures to eyeball.

KIND invariant
ARGS --system res/systems/solar_system.json --timeout 6 --atlas-dump 4000
ARGS --solar-log --startship v,res/ships/skiff_trim.json,Mars,flying-trim

# Earth: the page states "Diurnal temperature range: 283 K to 293 K" and an average of
# 288 K. The model is fitted on the NIGHT end, so 283.0 must come back exactly, and the day
# end is a PREDICTION the generator graded at +8.6 K. Pinned to a tenth: the tenth is what
# the dossier prints, and the identity check below is what catches the law moving.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+283\.0 K night to 30[01]\.[0-9] K day", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor\s+288\.0 K \(stated ground temperature, even share 0\.932\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# The Moon: no page states a ground temperature, so the anchor is the black-body its
# sunlight and albedo imply, and the ROW SAYS SO. Its 95.0 K night end is what the corpus
# states for the lunar equator; the 381 K day end is the model's own, 9 K under the 390 K
# the page states, which is the residual the generator prints and refuses to hide.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+95\.0 K night to 38[01]\.[0-9] K day", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor\s+27[01]\.[0-9] K \(DERIVED from this sun and albedo, even share 0\.015\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# Venus: the page states a range of about zero, so a swing of zero is AUTHORED DATA here --
# a stated 1.000, not the dash Mercury gets. That is #726's distinction made visible: the
# behaviour of an unpriced body and of a priced-no-swing body is the same uniform ground,
# but the STATE is not, and the row is where a reader sees which is which.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+737\.0 K night to 737\.0 K day", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Venus weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor\s+737\.0 K \(stated ground temperature, even share 1\.000\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Venus weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# Mercury states an average temperature and NOTHING about night and day, so there is no
# swing to print and the row dashes. Mars is the more interesting dash: its page DOES state
# a pair (184 K to 242 K, at the Viking 1 lander), and the fit refuses it -- the ends imply
# a day 265 K under this model while the page says 242 K, because a lander's afternoon is not
# a planet's subsolar point. Refusing to author is the honest answer, and the generator
# prints the residual (utils/ground_model.py).
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+-", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Mercury weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+-", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Mars weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# THE IDENTITY, on every swing the file printed. One knob and an anchor force the two ends:
# (day/T0)^4 + 3 (night/T0)^4 == 4, which is the same statement as "the sphere radiates
# exactly the star's absorbed power whatever the swing". The C++ that prints these ends and
# the python that fitted the knob are two spellings of one law, and this is the pass that
# catches them drifting -- the atmo.h / atmo_units.py arrangement (#636). Tolerance 1e-2
# because the dossier prints tenths of a kelvin.
CHECK all(abs((float(d) / float(a)) ** 4 + 3.0 * (float(n) / float(a)) ** 4 - 4.0) < 1e-2 for (n, d), a in zip(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) K night to ([\d.]+) K day", out), re.findall(r"ground anchor\s+([\d.]+) K", out)))
# ... and the identity is not vacuous: at least the three priced worlds are in that set.
CHECK len(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) K night to", out)) >= 3
# A dash must never arrive as a zero. 0.0 K of night would be a reading, and a 0.000 knob
# would be bare rock; both are numbers this file declines to invent (#727).
FORBID ground swing\s+0\.0 K night
FORBID even share 0\.000, anchor
# THE SAME REFUSAL, READ OUT. Mars's page DOES state a pair (184-242 K, at the Viking 1
# lander), and the fit refused it, so there is no knob and -- correctly -- no default: the
# log's underfoot column DASHES rather than printing the anchor as a "temperature here". A
# fallback of "no swing" would print 214.00 K at midnight next to a flat of 0 W/m2 and look
# like a reading; src/groundtemp.h says why there is no default to fall back to (#727).
CHECK all(d["body"] == "Mars" for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["underfoot_T"] is None and d["swing"] is None for d in solar)
# ... while the world's ONE authored ground reading is still there beside the dash, so the
# refusal costs a column and not the row.
CHECK all(d["ground_T"] is not None and abs(d["ground_T"] - 214.0) < 0.5 for d in solar)
