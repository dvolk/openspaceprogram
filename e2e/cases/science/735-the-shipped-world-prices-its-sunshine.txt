NAME 735-the-shipped-world-prices-its-sunshine
CLAIM The system the player actually flies answers the sun for every body that has a surface: each prints the light at its orbit, its Bond albedo with the words saying where that albedo came from, and the airless mean the two imply; the rows are one arithmetic rather than three printings; every swing names the rule that priced it; and no world is left without one -- a banded world's swing is its 1 bar level's, and the atlas uses a different word to say so.
KIND invariant
ARGS --system res/systems/ksp_system.json --timeout 6 --atlas-dump 4000
# #735: res/systems/ksp_system.json authored no albedo for any body, so render/736's
# starlight block -- built and pinned on solar_system.json, the file that DOES author
# them -- printed one row and three dashes on every world anyone actually loads. #749 then
# closed the rest of the list. Both halves are now one table in utils/gen_systems.py,
# KSP_ANALOGUE: which real world each shipped body is a draft of. The albedo and the swing
# are read through it out of solar_system.json rather than retyped (#636), so a body cannot
# be Mars for one figure and Venus for another, and there is no longer a per-body list of
# refusals to keep in step with the system. What remains visible is the PROVENANCE: borrowed,
# converted, or DEFAULT, in words beside the number, because 0.015 is the same digits whether
# the corpus's Moon page priced it or one of our rules filled a gap.
#
# Kerbin is the block to weigh, because all four of its rows are independently authored
# and still have to agree: the light follows from Kerbol's photosphere and Kerbin's own
# rail (#687), the albedo is Earth's 0.294 from the RSS sheets, the surface temperature
# is the 1976 table's sea-level row, and the gap is the subtraction between them.
EXPECT [atlas] Kerbin weight=
# 1. THE BLACK-BODY ROW FOLLOWS FROM THE TWO ABOVE IT (T = ((1-A) S / 4 sigma)^(1/4)).
#    The same relation render/736 pins on solar_system; here it runs on the shipped
#    file, which is the one whose albedos were missing. 0.05 K is print rounding.
CHECK (lambda m: abs(float(m.group(3)) - (float(m.group(1)) * (1.0 - float(m.group(2))) / 4.0 / 5.670374419e-8) ** 0.25) < 0.05)(re.search(r"sunlight \(mean orbit\)\s+([\d.]+) W/m2\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   bond albedo\s+([\d.]+)(?: \([^)]*\))?\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   black-body temp\s+([\d.]+) K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Kerbin weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)))
# 2. AND THE GAP FROM THE TWO BESIDE IT. Kerbin reads +33.0 K under 1361 W/m2, an albedo
#    of 0.294 and an authored 288.15 K -- which is Earth's own gap, arrived at by three
#    different authors: the star sized so Kerbin receives Earth's sun, Earth's albedo
#    taken by analogy, the ground's reading from the same 1976 table its air starts from.
#    Nothing in the sim models a greenhouse (#728 is parked on that), so the row reports
#    a difference and names the two numbers it is the difference of.
CHECK (lambda m: abs(float(m.group(3)) - (float(m.group(2)) - float(m.group(1)))) < 0.05)(re.search(r"black-body temp\s+([\d.]+) K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   surface temp\s+([\d.]+) K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   greenhouse gap\s+\+([\d.]+) K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Kerbin weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)))
CHECK (lambda m: 30.0 < float(m.group(1)) < 36.0)(re.search(r"greenhouse gap\s+\+([\d.]+) K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Kerbin weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)))
# 3. AN ALBEDO AND NO GROUND READING. Moho takes Mercury's 0.068, sits at
#    9087 W/m2 -- one of the two hottest places in the game, and the one place an albedo of
#    0.068 vs 0.11 is a visible 5 K -- and its airless mean prints 439.6 K, which is the
#    figure mercuryfact.html states for Mercury itself (it states irradiance 9082.7 and
#    Bond 0.068 there, and this law closes to 439.5, so the corpus's arithmetic and ours
#    are the same arithmetic). Its surface temp
#    and its gap dash, because this file authors no temperature for the bare rocks -- the
#    wiki's pair is a night/day extreme and averaging it is a design call (#687).
#    Bounds at 2% / 2 K rather than digits: the flux prints through %.4g, so a nudge past
#    10 kW/m2 would print 1e+04 and this pattern would die on the FORMAT, not the physics.
CHECK (lambda m: 8900.0 < float(m.group(1)) < 9280.0 and m.group(2) == "0.068" and 437.6 < float(m.group(3)) < 441.6)(re.search(r"sunlight \(mean orbit\)\s+([\d.e+]+) W/m2\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   bond albedo\s+([\d.]+)(?: \([^)]*\))?\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   black-body temp\s+([\d.]+) K[^\n]*\n\s*\[atlas\]   surface temp\s+-\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   greenhouse gap\s+-", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moho weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)))
# 4. A CONVERTED FIGURE SAYS SO, IN THE FILE PLAYERS LOAD. Minmus is ice, and the satellite
#    fact sheets state a visual GEOMETRICAL albedo and no Bond albedo. #749 bridges that with
#    the phase factor this same corpus implies for itself (make_solar_system.py:corpus_phase_
#    integral -- the median of the ten pages that state both, printed with the factor-of-three
#    spread the ten disagree by), and the marker rides through the loader into the dossier row
#    and through one more hop: Minmus borrows Enceladus, and Enceladus was converted before it
#    was borrowed. #736's rule -- Bond, never geometrical -- is intact: the row reads 0.681,
#    not Enceladus's geometric 1.00, and a reader can see which of the two it is. The closure
#    band here is 0.25 K rather than the 0.05 K Kerbin's row gets: this albedo prints to three
#    decimals (0.681 for a file value of 0.6814), and on a body that reflects 68% of the light
#    the remaining 32% is thin enough that a print rounding is a tenth of a kelvin.
CHECK (lambda m: abs(float(m.group(3)) - (float(m.group(1)) * (1.0 - float(m.group(2))) / 4.0 / 5.670374419e-8) ** 0.25) < 0.25)(re.search(r"sunlight \(parent's orbit\)\s+([\d.]+) W/m2\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   bond albedo\s+([\d.]+)\s*\n\s*\[atlas\]   black-body temp\s+([\d.]+) K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Minmus weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)))
# 5. THE LIGHT FALLS OFF AND THE ALBEDO DOES NOT CARE. Eeloo takes Pluto's 0.720 -- the
#    second brightest in this file after Eve's Venus-analogue 0.770 -- and sits 6.6x further
#    out than Kerbin, so its airless mean is the coldest reading in the system. Both rows
#    print from the same star, which is what makes the pair worth reading: 1361 W/m2 gives
#    255 K at Kerbin's rail, 31 W/m2 gives 79 K at Eeloo's.
CHECK (lambda e: float(re.search(r"sunlight \(mean orbit\)\s+([\d.]+) W/m2", e).group(1)) < 40.0 and float(re.search(r"black-body temp\s+([\d.]+) K", e).group(1)) < 90.0 and re.search(r"bond albedo\s+0\.720", e))(re.search(r"\[atlas\] Eeloo weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# 6. #742: THE SWING, ON THE FILE PLAYERS LOAD. Kerbin's two ends are not two free numbers:
#    one knob and one anchor force both, and the anchor stays the area-mean of the radiation
#    (src/groundtemp.h), so the three printed figures have to satisfy
#    (day/T0)^4 + 3 (night/T0)^4 = 4. Band 0.02 on the sum: each end prints to 0.1 K.
#    Kerbin's knob prints beside the anchor precisely so this is checkable by eye -- 0.932 is
#    Earth's, READ from solar_system.json through KSP_ANALOGUE, not retyped here (#636).
CHECK (lambda k: abs((float(re.search(r"to ([\d.]+) K", k).group(1)) / float(re.search(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+([\d.]+) K", k).group(1))) ** 4 + 3.0 * (float(re.search(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) to", k).group(1)) / float(re.search(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+([\d.]+) K", k).group(1))) ** 4 - 4.0) < 0.02)(re.search(r"\[atlas\] Kerbin weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# 7. #749: EVERY BORROWED KNOB NAMES THE WORLD IT CAME FROM, and the row that used to pin a
#    refusal now pins the borrowing. Duna's wiki pair is not a pair -- its max IS its mean, so
#    nothing here fits it (#742); its 0.708 is Mars's, and Mars's own is the least-squares fit
#    to a pair its stated mean contradicts (184-242 K implies a mean of ~203 K; its sheet says
#    214 K). Moho takes Mercury's airless floor -- and says so TWO hops deep: "via Mercury,
#    defaulted there". Both rows are our arithmetic, and both say so beside the ends.
CHECK (lambda d: re.search(r"ground swing\s+19[67]\.[0-9] to 25[01]\.[0-9] K", d) and re.search(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+214\.[0-9] K", d))(re.search(r"\[atlas\] Duna weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK (lambda m: re.search(r"ground swing\s+151\.0 to 620\.0 K", m) and re.search(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+439\.[0-9] K", m))(re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moho weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
#    And Jool, which used to be this file's one swing DASH. The dash came from a `bands` test
#    in the generators that refused to price a cloud deck; the surface-temp plan of
#    2026_11_12 deleted it on purpose, because this file authors a banded world's surface AT
#    the 1 bar, so a day/night pair there is a statement about that level rather than a
#    fiction. Jool now takes the same DEFAULT air-mass rule as its four real analogues in
#    solar_system.json -- 148.7 to 158.8 K around its air datum of 151.4 K -- and the atlas
#    keeps a different WORD for it (level, not ground), which is all that survived of the
#    refusal. Fit a pair for Jool instead of borrowing a default, or rename its row back to
#    "ground", and this check goes red.
CHECK (lambda j: re.search(r"level swing\s+148\.7 to 158\.8 K", j) and re.search(r"level anchor\s+151\.4 K", j) and re.search(r"air temp \(sea level\)\s+[\d.]+ K", j) and re.search(r"bond albedo\s+0\.343", j))(re.search(r"\[atlas\] Jool weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
FORBID bond albedo 0.000
