NAME 724-the-ground-prices-a-swing-when-a-source-or-a-rule-does
CLAIM A world's dossier carries its SWING -- the ground's coldest and hottest ends, the one knob that ties them, and the temperature they swing around -- and beside them the words saying where the knob came from: fitted from a stated pair, stated as having no range, borrowed by analogy, or a DEFAULT of ours. The two ends are not two free numbers: one knob forces both from the anchor, which is what makes this row arithmetic a test can check rather than two figures to eyeball.
KIND invariant
ARGS --system res/systems/solar_system.json --timeout 6 --atlas-dump 4000
ARGS --solar-log --startship v,res/ships/skiff_trim.json,Mars,flying-trim

# Earth: the page states "Diurnal temperature range: 283 to 293 K" and an average of
# 288 K. The model is fitted on the NIGHT end, so 283.0 must come back exactly, and the day
# end is a PREDICTION the generator graded at +8.6 K. Pinned to a tenth: the tenth is what
# the dossier prints, and the identity check below is what catches the law moving.
# The provenance is part of the row now (#749): "fitted from the night end" is the answer to
# "which end did you fit, and did the other one agree?", and it is the answer the generator
# already prints -- it just used to stop at the log.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+283\.0 to 30[01]\.[0-9] K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+288\.0 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# #793: on a world whose air lapses, that anchor is a SEA-LEVEL statement, and the label says so.
# 288.0 K is the coast; it is not the world's average ground and it is not what the Surface Map's
# coldest pixel costs. The row after it prices the same law at the top of the authored relief, so
# the dossier cannot quietly imply that one number covers a 7 km mountain. Both ends move together
# by the lapse x peak (283.0 - 42.4 = 240.6, 301.6 - 42.4 = 259.2): the height does not widen the
# swing, it shifts it, which is the whole claim of #793 in one pair of rows.
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor \(sea level\)\s+288\.0 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground at peak\s+240\.6 to 259\.2 K at 6\.5 km", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# The Moon: its page states a PAIR (95-390 K at the lunar equator) and no mean, so the anchor
# is solved from the pair and the row says so (surface.temperature_src: "SOLVED from the stated
# range 95.00-390.00 K, not a stated mean"). Both ends come back EXACT now, which is a strictly
# better pin than the old one: that honoured the night end and printed 381.2 K against the
# stated 390 K, a 9 K residual nobody read. If pair-solving dies, this row goes red.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+95\.0 to 390\.0 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+276\.5 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# #793 the other way: the Moon has no air to lapse through, so its anchor keeps the PLAIN label
# and gets no peak row at all. A world with nothing to say must not gain a parenthetical implying
# a distinction its file never drew (#726 keeps the absences apart).
CHECK "ground at peak" not in re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0)
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor\s+276\.5 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# Venus: the page states a range of about zero, so a swing of zero is AUTHORED DATA here --
# a stated 1.000, not a default. That is #726's distinction made visible, and #749 kept it
# intact: Mercury states NOTHING about night and day and now gets the airless floor as a
# DEFAULT, while Venus's stated no-range is labelled "stated: no range". Same ground, same
# behaviour, different STATE, and the row is where a reader sees which is which.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+737\.0 to 737\.0 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Venus weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+737\.0 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Venus weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+151\.2 to 620\.6 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Mercury weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# Mars is the interesting one, and it changed twice. Its page DOES state a pair (184 K to
# 242 K, at the Viking 1 lander). #742 refused that pair: one knob plus the page's own mean
# (214 K) forces a day/night RATIO, and 184-242 implies a mean nearer 203 K -- so the pair and
# the mean contradict each other by ~5% and no single knob honours all three. #749's answer is
# the least-squares fit to the PAIR (both ends miss by about the same amount, neither end
# invented) and the row naming itself: 196.3-250.5 K, "least-squares fit to the pair". Revert
# to honouring one end exactly, or drop the label, and this row goes red.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+19[0-9]\.[0-9] to 25[0-9]\.[0-9] K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Mars weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+214\.0 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Mars weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# A banded world prices a swing too now: this file authors its surface AT the 1 bar, so the
# two ends are two ends of that level, and the atlas keeps a different WORD for it (level, not
# ground) so the row cannot read as a sea floor nobody can find. Jupiter's knob is the DEFAULT
# air-mass rule, which says a 1-bar air meets itself night to day -- 162.0 to 173.1 K around a
# stated 165 K. The dash this row used to pin was the refusal that the surface-temp plan of
# 2026_11_12 deleted on purpose; the word "level" is what survived of it.
CHECK re.search(r"level swing\s+162\.0 to 173\.1 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Jupiter weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"level anchor\s+165\.0 K", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Jupiter weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# THE IDENTITY, on every swing the file printed. One knob and an anchor force the two ends:
# (day/T0)^4 + 3 (night/T0)^4 == 4, which is the same statement as "the sphere radiates
# exactly the star's absorbed power whatever the swing". The C++ that prints these ends and
# the python that fitted the knob are two spellings of one law, and this is the pass that
# catches them drifting -- the atmo.h / atmo_units.py arrangement (#636).
# The tolerance is NOT a constant, because it is rounding: the dossier prints tenths, so each
# end carries up to 0.05/a of relative error and the fourth powers multiply it by four. At a
# 288 K anchor that is nothing; at the coldest row in the file (a Kuiper body anchored at
# 10.6 K) the same half-decimal is 0.5% and the identity lands 0.05 off on arithmetic alone.
# So: 1.6/a, measured -- the worst row in this file is 1.003/a and the giants are 0.001/a. A
# real law change moves it by whole units, not hundredths.
CHECK len(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) to ([\d.]+) K", out)) == len(re.findall(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+([\d.]+) K", out))
CHECK all(abs((float(d) / float(a)) ** 4 + 3.0 * (float(n) / float(a)) ** 4 - 4.0) < 1.6 / float(a) for (n, d), a in zip(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) to ([\d.]+) K", out), re.findall(r"ground anchor(?: \(sea level\))?\s+([\d.]+) K", out)))
# ... and the identity is not vacuous, nor is any world left out: this file ships 92 rows --
# one star, four banded worlds that price a LEVEL swing, and 87 grounds that price a GROUND
# swing. That is the coverage claim from the surface-temp plan, read back off the dossier
# rather than asserted by the generator that made the file. (It read 85 until the seven comets
# of #786 landed priced grounds of their own; a corpus count quoted from a generator run is a
# number that goes stale on its own, which is why the CHECK says 87 and not "many".)
CHECK len(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) to", out)) == 87
CHECK len(re.findall(r"level swing\s+([\d.]+) to", out)) == 4
CHECK len(re.findall(r"(?:ground|level) swing\s+-", out)) == 0
# A dash must never arrive as a zero. 0.0 K of night would be a reading, and a 0.000 knob
# would be bare rock; both are numbers this file declines to invent (#727).
FORBID ground swing\s+0\.0 to
FORBID even share 0\.000, anchor
# THE OTHER HALF OF #749, READ OUT. Mars used to be the case that pinned a refusal: its pair
# was refused, so --solar-log dashed the underfoot column. Now the pair has a knob, so the
# column prints, and what is pinned is that it prints INSIDE the model's own ends rather than
# as the anchor dressed up as "the temperature here". The world's one authored ground reading
# (214 K, the page's average) is still a separate column beside it: the file's reading and our
# model's answer to "how hot is the ground under this hull right now" are two different
# questions, and #687 wants both visible rather than merged.
CHECK all(d["body"] == "Mars" for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["swing"] is not None and abs(d["swing"] - 0.708) < 1e-3 for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["underfoot_T"] is not None and 196.0 < d["underfoot_T"] < 251.0 for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["ground_T"] is not None and abs(d["ground_T"] - 214.0) < 0.5 for d in solar)
# A world with no swing at all still dashes this column rather than answering with its anchor:
# science/727 pins that on a body nothing anchors.
