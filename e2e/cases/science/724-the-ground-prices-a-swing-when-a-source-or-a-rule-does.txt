NAME 724-the-ground-prices-a-swing-when-a-source-or-a-rule-does
CLAIM A world's dossier carries its SWING -- the ground's coldest and hottest ends, the one knob that ties them, and the temperature they swing around -- and beside them the words saying where the knob came from: fitted from a stated pair, stated as having no range, borrowed by analogy, or a DEFAULT of ours. The two ends are not two free numbers: one knob forces both from the anchor, which is what makes this row arithmetic a test can check rather than two figures to eyeball.
KIND invariant
ARGS --system res/systems/solar_system.json --timeout 6 --atlas-dump 4000
ARGS --solar-log --startship v,res/ships/skiff_trim.json,Mars,flying-trim

# Earth: the page states "Diurnal temperature range: 283 K to 293 K" and an average of
# 288 K. The model is fitted on the NIGHT end, so 283.0 must come back exactly, and the day
# end is a PREDICTION the generator graded at +8.6 K. Pinned to a tenth: the tenth is what
# the dossier prints, and the identity check below is what catches the law moving.
# The provenance is part of the row now (#749): "fitted from the night end" is the answer to
# "which end did you fit, and did the other one agree?", and it is the answer the generator
# already prints -- it just used to stop at the log.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+283\.0 K night to 30[01]\.[0-9] K day \(fitted from the night end\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor\s+288\.0 K \(stated ground temperature, even share 0\.932\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Earth weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# The Moon: no page states a ground temperature, so the anchor is the black-body its
# sunlight and albedo imply, and the ROW SAYS SO. Its 95.0 K night end is what the corpus
# states for the lunar equator; the 381 K day end is the model's own, 9 K under the 390 K
# the page states, which is the residual the generator prints and refuses to hide.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+95\.0 K night to 38[01]\.[0-9] K day \(fitted from the night end\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor\s+27[01]\.[0-9] K \(DERIVED from this sun and albedo, even share 0\.015\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Moon weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# Venus: the page states a range of about zero, so a swing of zero is AUTHORED DATA here --
# a stated 1.000, not a default. That is #726's distinction made visible, and #749 kept it
# intact: Mercury states NOTHING about night and day and now gets the airless floor as a
# DEFAULT, while Venus's stated no-range is labelled "stated: no range". Same ground, same
# behaviour, different STATE, and the row is where a reader sees which is which.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+737\.0 K night to 737\.0 K day \(stated: no range\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Venus weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor\s+737\.0 K \(stated ground temperature, even share 1\.000\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Venus weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+154\.6 K night to 620\.5 K day \(DEFAULT airless floor\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Mercury weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# Mars is the interesting one, and it changed twice. Its page DOES state a pair (184 K to
# 242 K, at the Viking 1 lander). #742 refused that pair: one knob plus the page's own mean
# (214 K) forces a day/night RATIO, and 184-242 implies a mean nearer 203 K -- so the pair and
# the mean contradict each other by ~5% and no single knob honours all three. #749's answer is
# the least-squares fit to the PAIR (both ends miss by about the same amount, neither end
# invented) and the row naming itself: 196.3-250.5 K, "least-squares fit to the pair". Revert
# to honouring one end exactly, or drop the label, and this row goes red.
CHECK re.search(r"ground swing\s+19[0-9]\.[0-9] K night to 25[0-9]\.[0-9] K day \(least-squares fit to the pair\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Mars weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
CHECK re.search(r"ground anchor\s+214\.0 K \(stated ground temperature, even share 0\.708\)", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Mars weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# A banded giant has NO ground to swing, and that stays a dash after #749 -- it is a fact about
# the body, not a gap in the file. The dossier's label changes too (level, not ground: #738).
CHECK re.search(r"level swing\s+-", re.search(r"\[atlas\] Jupiter weight=.*?(?=\[atlas\] \w+ weight=|\Z)", out, re.S).group(0))
# THE IDENTITY, on every swing the file printed. One knob and an anchor force the two ends:
# (day/T0)^4 + 3 (night/T0)^4 == 4, which is the same statement as "the sphere radiates
# exactly the star's absorbed power whatever the swing". The C++ that prints these ends and
# the python that fitted the knob are two spellings of one law, and this is the pass that
# catches them drifting -- the atmo.h / atmo_units.py arrangement (#636). Tolerance 4e-2:
# the dossier prints tenths of a kelvin, and the identity's fourth powers multiply that
# rounding by four. Measured on this file: the worst row is Ariel at a 58.8 K anchor, where
# 0.1 K of print rounding is 0.17% and the sum lands 0.016 off; the giants' rows are an order
# of magnitude tighter. A real law change moves this by whole units, not hundredths.
CHECK len(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) K night to ([\d.]+) K day", out)) == len(re.findall(r"ground anchor\s+([\d.]+) K", out))
CHECK all(abs((float(d) / float(a)) ** 4 + 3.0 * (float(n) / float(a)) ** 4 - 4.0) < 4e-2 for (n, d), a in zip(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) K night to ([\d.]+) K day", out), re.findall(r"ground anchor\s+([\d.]+) K", out)))
# ... and the identity is not vacuous: #749 priced a swing for every body in this file that has
# a ground, which is 26 of its 31 rows. The five that stay dashed are the four banded giants
# and the star.
CHECK len(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+([\d.]+) K night to", out)) >= 26
CHECK len(re.findall(r"ground swing\s+-", out)) == 0
# A dash must never arrive as a zero. 0.0 K of night would be a reading, and a 0.000 knob
# would be bare rock; both are numbers this file declines to invent (#727).
FORBID ground swing\s+0\.0 K night
FORBID even share 0\.000, anchor
# THE OTHER HALF OF #749, READ OUT. Mars used to be the case that pinned a refusal: its pair
# was refused, so --solar-log dashed the underfoot column. Now the pair has a knob, so the
# column prints, and what is pinned is that it prints INSIDE the model's own ends rather than
# as the anchor dressed up as "the temperature here". The world's one authored ground reading
# (214 K, the page's average) is still a separate column beside it: the file's reading and our
# model's answer to "how hot is the ground under this hull right now" are two different
# questions, and #687 wants both visible rather than merged.
CHECK all(d["body"] == "Mars" for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["swing"] is not None and abs(d["swing"] - 0.708) < 1e-3 for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["underfoot_T"] is not None and 196.0 < d["underfoot_T"] < 251.0 for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["ground_T"] is not None and abs(d["ground_T"] - 214.0) < 0.5 for d in solar)

# science/727 pins that on Himalia, a catalogue moon nothing anchors.
