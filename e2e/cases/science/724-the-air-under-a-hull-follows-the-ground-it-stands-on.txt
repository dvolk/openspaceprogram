NAME 724-the-air-under-a-hull-follows-the-ground-it-stands-on
CLAIM Where a world authors an air-follow knob, the air at a hull is the profile's own temperature moved toward the ground's by exactly the knob times the weight the column itself supplies; where a world authors none, the air keeps the profile even beside a ground hundreds of kelvin away.
KIND invariant
ARGS --system res/systems/ksp_system.json --timeout 5 --solar-log --startship v,res/ships/skiff.json,Kerbin,flying-cruise
# #724 rung D. Kerbin is one of the two worlds utils/gen_systems.py prices a coupling for
# (KSP_AIR_FOLLOW; the value 0.350 is INVENTED -- no source states how far an air follows its
# ground -- and the generator prints that word rather than dressing it in an analogy). The
# row's four temperature columns are one law and not four readings:
#
#   air_T = prof_T + follow * w * (underfoot_T - prof_T)
#
# air_T is MEASURED at the hull (src/tick.cpp prints the aero pass's own lastAirT, the number
# the Mach number divides by -- a recomputed second authority over one fact is #687's
# complaint). prof_T is what the authored profile says at this altitude, underfoot_T is rung
# C's ground, and w is the air's own pressure fraction p(alt)/p(0): the air follows the ground
# WHERE THE AIR IS THICK, which is one fewer invented number than naming a boundary-layer
# depth. A cruise bed at ~8.5 km is where this is worth pinning, because w = 0.33 there and a
# coupling that ignored the column is measurably wrong; on a pad w = 0.97 and it would not be.
#
# 1. THE COUPLING IS LIVE AND POINTS THE RIGHT WAY. The ground at this local time is warmer
#    than the profile, and the air sits between the two -- not at either end.
CHECK all(d["air_T"] is not None and d["prof_T"] is not None for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["prof_T"] < d["air_T"] < d["underfoot_T"] for d in solar if d["underfoot_T"] > d["prof_T"])
CHECK all(d["air_T"] - d["prof_T"] > 1.0 for d in solar)
# 2. THE IDENTITY, closed on the row's own columns. 0.350 is the authored knob, so this check
#    is what says the mechanism is the file's and not the model's opinion. Band 0.05 K: the
#    hull's COM and the log's root position differ by tens of metres, which moves prof_T and w
#    a little, and the columns print to 0.01 K.
CHECK all(abs(d["air_T"] - (d["prof_T"] + 0.350 * d["w"] * (d["underfoot_T"] - d["prof_T"]))) < 0.05 for d in solar)
# 3. THE COLUMN DECIDES THE WEIGHT. 8.5 km up, a third of the sea-level air is overhead, so a
#    third of the coupling applies. Ignoring w -- coupling at full strength anywhere in the
#    air -- reads 0.350 here and reddens check 2; a coupling that stopped at the datum would
#    read w = 1 at a pad and reddens here.
CHECK all(0.20 < d["w"] < 0.45 for d in solar)
CHECK all(d["air_T"] - d["prof_T"] < 0.350 * (d["underfoot_T"] - d["prof_T"]) + 0.05 for d in solar)
# 4. THE GROUND UNDER THE HULL STILL ANSWERS TO ITS OWN KNOB, unchanged by the air under it.
#    Its anchor is an AUTHORED reading (Kerbin states 288.15 K), never this coupling's output:
#    an air warmed by a ground whose anchor is that air's own datum would define its
#    temperature in terms of itself -- the loop #724's review flagged, and the reason rung D
#    reads the anchor off authored numbers only.
CHECK all(abs(d["ground_T"] - 288.15) < 0.01 for d in solar)
CHECK all(abs((d["underfoot_T"] / 288.15) ** 4 - (0.9323 + (1.0 - 0.9323) * 4.0 * max(0.0, d["flat"] / d["flux"]))) < 0.02 for d in solar if d["flux"])
FORBID air_T=-
FORBID prof_T=-
